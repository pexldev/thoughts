## The Wednesday voice — one line

**A man brings the one story he couldn't digest this week, tries to extract its meaning, fails honestly, and hands the question to the audience.**

Story time with a guy who overthinks. NOT a lecture. NOT an essay. NOT a life lesson. The host is never the teacher — he's the confused one asking for help. The show's title is the format's voice: "I could be wrong" is what a man says mid-digestion, not post-sermon.

## The Five Laws — derived from the corpus (survivors vs hall_of_shame, July 2026 audit)

### Law 1 — It happened TO ME, THIS WEEK
Every surviving episode is one concrete, recent, first-person incident: dad called at 6:30 AM (13_dad), friend demanded the car for six days (11_hyundai), cousin + mom + eye-contact feedback (14_eye_contact). Every hall_of_shame episode is a THEME: freedom (1_cage), masculinity (6_be_a_man), hope-via-metaphor (9_omlette), fame psychology (12_other_guy) — or someone else's story (15_uncle, secondhand, narrated not lived).
**Test:** can you name the day and place it happened to you? "I've been thinking about X" = a Friday thesis, not a Wednesday episode.

### Law 2 — A second human with quoted dialogue (the antagonist rule)
Survivors have live characters speaking: dad gets 5 quoted lines, the Hyundai friend's demands are performed verbatim, eye_contact has FOUR speaking characters. Every flop has the host alone: with a parrot (cage), with eggs (omlette — not one other human speaks in the whole script), with his phone (AI_ads), with an imaginary man (other_guy).
**Rule:** at least one real back-and-forth exchange in the first 90 seconds. No dialogue = essay = flop.

### Law 3 — Keep the stakes petty (altitude check)
Survivors: a hatchback, a phone call, a coffee order, a birthday. Shame: freedom, hope, what-is-a-man. High-altitude topics force conclusions — that's where preachiness comes from, at topic selection, before a word is written. Petty stakes force stories. Philosophy may LEAK from the petty story ("that's their love language"); it may never lead it.

### Law 4 — End on a joke-wrapped feeling or ONE real question — never a stated meaning
Survivors end: "Could this have been a text?" (dad), "we'll all be back here next week" (bday). Shame ends: "I think that's hope" (omlette), "everyone has their own story" (cage), the meaning-menu (uncle: "maybe courage, maybe family, maybe OG").
**Rules:** the feeling rides inside the final joke. If closing on a question, it's the ONE question the host genuinely can't answer ("Why do I need people around me and he doesn't?") — asked plainly, left open. Never a menu of meanings. The episode must end genuinely unresolved — that's also what makes "tell me in the comments" honest instead of a CTA.

### Law 5 — The self-turn is mandatory, but late and brief
The confession beat (press conference for diarrhea; "guilty as charged") lands in the back half. Front-load the story, not the introspection. Introspection-first is how 12_other_guy flopped.

## The preach test — run on every draft

Flag any sentence a LinkedIn post could contain. Then convert, don't just cut:
- **Advice to the viewer** ("don't fall for this") → say it to a CHARACTER in the story instead, or convert to confession ("I fall for this weekly")
- **Universal claim** ("people only talk to you when they need something") → replace with what happened ("he asked kaisa chal raha hai; ten seconds later I was looking at belts")
- **Thesis lines** are allowed ONLY as something that happened to the host or scares the host — never as a finding about people

## Why anyone watches — packaging brief (hand to /packaging)

1. **Story hook** — the wait-WHAT fact goes in the first 5 seconds AND the title. Title the STORY, never the theme: "Bad People" ❌ → "He Offered 50% Less and Called It a Favour" ✅. Theme titles promise sermons; story titles promise gossip. People click gossip.
2. **They stay for the relatability spiral** — "yaar yeh toh main hoon" (brand brief's own words). The self-turns ARE the product, not relief between insights.
3. **They comment because he genuinely asks** — only works if Law 4 held and the episode truly ends unresolved.

## The harvest connection

Incident episodes produce cuttable Shorts — dialogue scenes with designated recipients (see `/wednesday-reels`). Essay episodes structurally produce nothing cuttable: no scene, no recipient — which is why hall_of_shame episodes also failed as reels. **Outline check:** if you can't point at 3 cut-worthy dialogue moments in the outline, the episode is an essay wearing a story's clothes. Restructure before writing.
