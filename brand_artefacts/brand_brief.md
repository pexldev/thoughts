## What This Podcast Is

An audio podcast by one guy who thinks too much and decided to record it. No structure, no preaching, no life advice from someone who has it figured out. The show is raw, honest, self-deprecating, and deeply relatable — the kind of content where the listener finishes an episode thinking "yaar yeh toh main hoon."

Primary audience: Indian urban millennials. Corporate professionals. Mumbai/metro crowd. People who overthink and find that funny.

## The Logo

### What It Is
An ornate, beautifully detailed mirror frame with cracked glass inside.

### What We Need
- A minimal line-art illustration of an ornate mirror — think vintage, slightly baroque frame. The kind of frame that belongs in an old mansion.
- The glass inside is cracked. One clean crack running through it — not shattered, not dramatic. Just cracked. Like it happened once and nobody bothered to fix it.
- The illustration should work as: a standalone logo, a circular DP crop, and a small watermark overlay on video/reels.
- Line art only. No fills, no gradients inside the illustration. The frame can have subtle detail but should remain minimal enough to read at small sizes.

### Rationale
The show's entire premise is relatability — people listen and see themselves in it. A mirror is the most direct expression of that. You look into it expecting to see something polished and put-together, and instead you see a crack.

That crack is doing a lot of work:
- It signals **raw and unpolished** — nothing here is perfect
- It signals **lost** — something broke and we're okay with that
- It signals **fun** — there's a quiet dark humor in a beautiful frame holding broken glass
- The contrast between the ornate frame and the cracked glass is the whole show in one image — it could've been polished, but it isn't, and that's the point

Nobody else has this mark. It's ownable, memorable, and the meaning lands without any explanation.

## Color

### Primary Color
**Acid Yellow**

Hex reference range: `#D4FF00` to `#CCFF00`  
Think: the yellow of a highlighter, a caution sign, something slightly radioactive. Not gold, not pastel yellow. Acid. Loud.

### Supporting Colors
- **Off-white / Cream** — `#F5F0E8` — for text and logo on dark backgrounds. Not pure white.
- **Charcoal / Near-black** — `#1A1A1A` — for text on light applications.
- The acid yellow is the only "color." Everything else is neutral.

### Rationale
The brand words are raw, unassuming, lost — which might suggest a muted, understated palette. But the show is also **fun and curious**, and the audience is Mumbai millennials who grew up on loud, unapologetic visual culture.

Acid yellow is the right contradiction:
- It's **unexpected** — nobody associates yellow with a thinking/introspective podcast. That's exactly why it works.
- It's **unassuming in attitude but loud in presence** — like the show itself. Doesn't preach, but you notice it.
- It's **retro without being vintage** — acid yellow has an almost 90s energy, slightly unhinged, which matches the "lost but self-aware" personality
- It **pops on any background** — works on black, white, dark photography, video thumbnails
- It gives the brand a **single ownable color** — you'll know it's Side Effects of Thinking from color alone before you read the name

Reference mood: Bobby Lee's visual energy — loud, warm, slightly chaotic, never corporate.

## Typography

### Direction
Bold, condensed, slightly imperfect. Not handwritten — but not sterile either. Something that feels like it was set by someone who was overthinking where to put it.

### References to explore
- **Archivo Black** — free, bold, condensed. Good starting point.
- **Space Grotesk** — slightly quirky, modern but not corporate
- **Syne** — has personality without being loud about it

Avoid: anything serif, anything that reads as "intellectual podcast", anything too clean or too startup-y.

### Tagline
*Think. Overthink. Repeat.*  
Always lowercase or all-caps — never title case. Set smaller than the show name. Never bold.

## Poster Layout

```
[Acid yellow background]

[Cracked mirror logo mark — centered, upper half]

SIDE EFFECTS OF THINKING
think. overthink. repeat.
```

- Show name: large, bold, condensed — centered
- Tagline: small, below the name, same centerline
- Nothing else. No social handles, no episode info, no extra elements.
- The logo mark sits above the text with breathing room.

---

## What To Avoid

- Brain icons, lightbulbs, thought bubbles — too obvious
- Orange (previous brand color — retired)
- Pill / medicine box references — retired concept
- Gradients
- Drop shadows
- Anything that looks like a corporate startup's podcast
- Overly clean or symmetrical layouts
- Serif fonts
- Pure white or pure black backgrounds
