# Mascot Brief — Side Effects of Thinking
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** May 21, 2026  
**Prepared for:** Illustrator  
**Style reference:** Tiger Belly podcast mascot, Bad Friends cartoon characters

---

## What The Mascot Is

A cartoon character loosely based on the host. Not a caricature, not a portrait — somewhere in between. Think the way Tiger Belly's mascot is clearly Bobby Lee but also its own thing. The character should be immediately recognisable to anyone who's seen the host, but work completely independently as a standalone character.

This is the face of the show. The mirror with the cracked glass is the logo. This character is the personality.

---

## Physical Description

Base the character loosely on the reference photos provided. Key features to carry into the cartoon:

**Must have (signature features):**
- **Round metal-frame glasses** — this is the single most important feature. Round, thin frame. This is what makes him instantly recognisable. Consider having one lens with a hairline crack — subtle callback to the podcast's mirror logo.
- **Full beard** — natural, not groomed-to-perfection. It grew because he was overthinking and forgot to trim it.
- **Slim, tall build** — lean, not athletic. The kind of guy who forgets to eat sometimes.
- **Short dark hair** — slightly unkempt. Not styled, just... there.

**Face shape:** Oval, slightly long. Dark complexion.

---

## What He's Wearing

Mumbai corporate casual — the uniform of someone who had a job once and still dresses like it out of habit.

- Plain t-shirt or a slightly wrinkled button-down shirt, half-tucked
- Regular trousers or jeans — nothing that says "I tried"
- No tie, no blazer, no sneakers that cost money
- Barefoot or chappals would be very on-brand (he recorded in pajamas, he knows who he is)

Avoid: anything that looks cool, expensive, or put-together. He is not that guy.

---

## The Expression

This is the most important part.

**Default face:** Mid-thought. Not happy, not sad. That specific expression when you're 4 layers deep into a thought and someone asks "you okay?" and you say "haan haan" but you're clearly somewhere else entirely.

Eyes slightly unfocused behind the glasses. Mouth in a neutral line, maybe a slight upward twitch — like he's about to say something but decided not to.

This is not a sad character. He's not depressed. He's just... processing. Always processing.

**What to avoid:** Big smile, thumbs up, excited pose — anything that looks like a brand ambassador. He is not selling you anything.

---

## The Pose

No fixed action pose — this character is caught mid-existence, not mid-action.

Options the illustrator can explore:
- Standing with hands loosely in pockets, looking slightly to the side
- Sitting, chin resting on hand — but casually, not the Rodin Thinker statue pose
- Walking but clearly not paying attention to where he's going

**Signature detail to include somewhere:** A small crack. Could be on one lens of his glasses, on his phone screen if he's holding one, or on a chai cup in his hand. One subtle crack. Ties back to the mirror logo without being obvious.

---

## Style Direction

**Reference:** Tiger Belly podcast mascot, Bad Friends cartoon characters — exaggerated but human, cartoon but not childish.

- Bold outlines
- Flat colour fills, no complex shading
- Slightly exaggerated proportions — head a little bigger, limbs a little looser
- The style should feel like something drawn by someone who is good at drawing but doesn't want you to know that

**Colour palette for the character:**
- Skin tone: warm brown/medium dark
- Outfit: muted, neutral colours — grey, navy, white. Nothing loud.
- The character itself should NOT be acid yellow — that's the background/brand color. He lives on top of it.
- Glasses frame: simple dark or gold-tinted thin metal

---

## Where The Mascot Will Be Used

- Instagram profile posts and stories
- YouTube thumbnail corner element
- Watermark/sticker version for Reels
- Potential merch (sticker, t-shirt)
- Episode artwork

Design it to work at small sizes — if it loses all character when shrunk to a sticker, it's too detailed.

---

## What To Avoid

- Making him look cool or aspirational
- Big expressions — no wide grins, no shocked faces
- Superhero posture — no chest out, no power stance
- Anything that looks like a startup founder or influencer
- Overly cute or childish style (this is not a children's show)
- Too much detail that disappears at small sizes

---

## The One-Line Brief

*A tall, bearded, bespectacled Mumbai guy who is permanently mid-thought — cartoon style, slightly exaggerated, one cracked lens, dressed like he had somewhere to be and then forgot.*
