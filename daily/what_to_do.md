# Daily Exercises

---

## Exercise 1 — The Why Ladder *(10 min)*

Pick literally anything.

> "My office chair."

Now ask **Why does this exist?**

Answer. Then ask **Why?** again. Repeat 10 times.

**Example**

Office chair.

↓ Because sitting comfortably matters.

↓ Why? Because humans work for long hours.

↓ Why? Because society values productivity.

↓ Why? Because...

Eventually you'll land somewhere philosophical.

This teaches you to dig **vertically** instead of horizontally.

---

## Exercise 2 — One Observation → Five Angles *(10 min)*

Take one observation.

> People clap when planes land.

Now force yourself to find five explanations:

- evolutionary
- economic
- relationship
- absurd
- personal

**Example**

- Maybe they're thanking the pilot.
- Maybe they're relieved they didn't die.
- Maybe everyone wants closure.
- Maybe humans clap because silence is uncomfortable.
- Maybe we clap because nobody knows how to leave an emotional situation.

Now you've created five podcast directions.

---

## Exercise 3 — Analogy Gym *(5 min)*

Pick two unrelated objects.

> traffic signal + marriage

Now connect them.

> Marriage is basically a traffic signal.
> Everyone is waiting for green.
> Nobody knows who made the rules.
> Half the people jump the signal anyway.

Do this with random words every day.

After a few months your brain automatically starts seeing analogies.

---

## Exercise 4 — The "Keep Going" Drill *(5 min)*

Set a timer. Choose one sentence.

> Why do people buy SUVs?

Now you're forbidden to change topics. Keep talking for five minutes.

Whenever you feel like changing — ask **"What else?"**

This is probably the single best exercise for improv.

---

## Exercise 5 — Reverse Engineer Reality

Throughout the day, whenever something surprises you — don't ask *What happened?*

Ask: **What belief did I just discover?**

**Examples**

| Event | Underlying belief |
|---|---|
| Friend lied. | Immediate comfort is more valuable than honesty. |
| Someone bought a ₹30 lakh car. | Status is worth paying interest for. |
| Your manager promised something impossible. | Future problems belong to Future Me. |

Now you're collecting **theses**, not stories.

---

## My Favourite Exercise *(the one I'd actually make you do)*

Every night record 10 minutes with no preparation.

**Prompt:** *"What's the most interesting thing I noticed today?"*

**Rules:**

- No notes.
- No editing.
- No restarting.
- Whenever you stop, ask yourself:
  - Why?
  - What does that remind me of?
  - What's another example?
  - What's the opposite?
  - What if everyone behaved like this?

---

Over time you'll notice your brain developing a rhythm that looks like this:

```
Observation
    ↓
Pattern
    ↓
Analogy
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Counterargument
    ↓
Joke
    ↓
Bigger idea
```

That's exactly how strong conversational comedians think on stage.
