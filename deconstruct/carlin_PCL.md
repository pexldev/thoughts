##  5th loop
- ***opens a loop with a statement***
    Let’s get to some of these other non-victims. 

- ***Acknowledges the audience / Transitions***
    You probably noticed elsewhere I used the word fat. 

- ***States his thesis immediately***
    I used that word because that’s what fat people are. They’re fat. 

- ***Enumeration / Elimination + Semantic Deconstruction***
    They’re not large; they’re not stout, chunky, hefty, or plump. And they’re not big-boned. 
    ***Unexpected Analogy*** Dinosaurs are big-boned. 
    These people are not necessarily obese either. 
    ***Definition***
    Obese is a medical term. And they’re not overweight. Overweight implies there is some correct weight. There is no correct weight. Heavy is also a misleading term
    
    ***Absurd Comparison***An aircraft carrier is heavy, it is not fat. 
    ***Repetition / Reinforcement*** Only people are fat. That’s what fat people are. They’re fat. 
    I offer no apology for this. 
    ***Preemptive Defense***
    It is not intended as criticism or insult. It is simply descriptive language. 
    ***Restates Principle***
    I don’t like euphemisms. ***power statement***Euphemisms are a form of lying. Fat people are not gravitationally disadvantaged. They’re fat. I prefer seeing things the way they are. Not the way some people wish they were.

### The bigger pattern

    This whole section follows one structure:

    Claim

    → Reject Alternatives

    → Define Terms

    → Give Analogies

    → State Philosophy

##  6th loop

- ***Open loop with a claim***

    I don’t believe groups deserve extra-special names. For instance, midgets and dwarfs are midgets and dwarfs. They are not little people. 
    
- ***Enumeration / Elimination + Semantic Deconstruction ***
    ***Absurd Comparison*** Infants are little people; leprechauns are little people.
    ***repeats*** - Midgets and dwarfs are midgets and dwarfs. 
    ***Empathy / Concession***
    They don’t get any taller by calling them little people. I wish their lives were different. 
    ***Visual Illustration***
    I wish they didn’t have to go around starring at other peoples crotches, but I can’t fix that. 
    ***Returns to thesis***
    And I’m not going to lie about what they are. The politically sensitive commandos would probably like me to call them “vertically challenged”. 
    ***Elimination*** They are not vertically challenged. 
    ***Absurd Comparison*** A skydiver is vertically challenged. The person who designed the empire state building was vertically challenged. 
    ***Button*** Midgets and dwarfs are midgets and dwarfs.

            Claim

            → Eliminate competing definitions

            → Humanize the people

            → Return to the central argument

            → Disprove another competing definition

            → Use absurd analogy as evidence

            → Restate thesis (Button)


##  7th loop
- ***Open loop with a claim***

    Also, crippled people are crippled. 

- ***Enumeration / Elimination + Semantic Deconstruction***

    They are not differently-abled. 
    
    **Hypothetical Premise - Fine. Let's assume you're right.** 
    
    If you insist on using such tortured language as differently-abled, 
    
    **Logical Extension**

    then you must use it on all of us. 
    
    **Reframing with evidence + Escalation + Self-deprecating Absurdity**
    
    We’re all differently-abled. You can do things I can’t do. I can do things you can’t do. I can pick my nose with my thumb. I can switch hands while masturbating and gain a stroke. We are all differently-abled.

    Crippled people are crippled. It’s a perfectly honorable word. There is no shame in it.

    It’s in the Bible: “Jesus healed the cripples”. 
    
    **Concrete Example + Contrast + Exaggeration + Button**

    He didn’t “engage in rehabilitative strategies for the physically disadvantaged”.

## 8th Loop

- ***opens loop with a statement***

    So, let me tell you how I handle some of these speech issues. 

- ***provides evidence to the statement***
    
    First of all, I say “black”. I say “black” because most black people prefer “black”. 

- ***absurd analogies***

    I don’t say “people of color”. People of color sounds like something you see when you are on mushrooms. Besides, the use of people of color is dishonest. It means precisely the same thing as colored people. If you’re not willing to say “colored people” you shouldn’t be saying “people of color”.

    - ***opens another loop within 8th loop***
    
        Besides, the whole idea of color is bullshit anyway. What should we call white people? “People of no color”? 

    - ***Literal Interpretation / Semantic Deconstruction***

        Isn’t pink a color? In fact, white people aren’t really white at all, they’re different shades of pink, olive and beige. 
            
    - ***Logical Reclassification***

        In other words, they’re colored. 

    - ***Reverse Examination***

        And black people are rarely black. I see mostly different shades of brown and tan. 

    - ***Evidence through observation***

        In fact, some light-skinned black people are lighter than the darkest white people. Look how dark the people in India are. They’re dark brown but they’re still considered white people. 

    - ***Asks Q***

        What’s going on here? 

    - **Button/Release**

        May I see the color chart? 

    - **Return to Thesis**

        “People of color” is an awkward, bullshit , liberal-guilt phrase that obscures meaning rather than enhancing it. Shall we call fat people, “people of size”?
