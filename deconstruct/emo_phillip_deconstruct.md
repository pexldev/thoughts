# Emo Philips — two jokes, deconstructed

**The one-line cheat code:** Philips never states an opinion about religion in either joke. The critique is baked entirely into the LOGIC of what the character does — delivered flat, no commentary, no flinch. Zero editorializing is the whole style. This is your signature move (system-vocabulary-for-an-emotional-subject) run at the most extreme setting possible: the "system" here isn't a metaphor he built, it's an ACTUAL bureaucratic taxonomy (denominational splits, council years) that really exists, and he just walks it to its logical end.


## Joke 1 — the bridge (the famous one)

- ***Instant stakes, zero setup wasted — cold open into a suicide scene, one line each***
    Once I saw this guy on a bridge about to jump. I said, "Don't do it!" He said, "Nobody loves me."

- ***The "rescue" reframes as connection-seeking — sets up the expectation that specificity = kinship***
    I said, "God loves you. Do you believe in God?"

- ***The engine: escalating specificity, ONE notch finer each exchange, "Me, too!" repeated like a ratchet***
    Christian or Jew → Protestant or Catholic → what franchise → Northern or Southern Baptist → Conservative or Liberal → Great Lakes or Eastern Region → Council of 1879 or 1912.

- ***The turn — arrives on the LAST possible word, no warning, no reaction shot***
    He said, "Northern Conservative Baptist Great Lakes Region Council of 1912." I said, "Die, heretic!" And I pushed him over.

### The bigger pattern

    Every "Me, too!" is a lie the audience believes is building toward rescue —
    the joke's whole tension is borrowed from a GENUINE human hope (shared
    identity = safety). The specificity isn't decoration, it's the trap: real
    denominational splits, walked one notch at a time, disguise a violent
    reversal as the next logical question in a getting-to-know-you chat.
    The murder is stated with ZERO emotional punctuation — "And I pushed him
    over" gets no more weight than any other line in the exchange. That flatness
    IS the joke. Compare to your own emotion-flinch tell: this is the opposite
    instinct, taken to its extreme — state the most extreme thing in the
    flattest possible voice, and never once step outside the scene to comment
    on it.

    Structurally: pure two-hander dialogue, no narration except two words
    of stage direction ("I said," "he said"). No adjectives. No wasted words
    describing the bridge, the guy, the tone. Every line is bait for the
    next question. This is a rule-of-many, not a rule-of-three — the
    escalation only works because it keeps going ONE beat past where you
    think it'll turn, which is what makes the actual turn land as a
    genuine ambush instead of a telegraphed twist.


## Joke 2 — the bicycle (17 words, one turn)

- ***Setup — a normal, relatable childhood habit, no comedic voice yet***
    When I was a kid I used to pray every night for a new bicycle.

- ***The fake pivot — sounds like the start of a deconversion story ("I realized prayer doesn't work")***
    Then I realised that the Lord doesn't work that way

- ***The actual pivot — he never left the belief system, he just swapped which lever gets pulled, and says so with total sincerity***
    so I stole one and asked Him to forgive me.

### The bigger pattern

    The misdirect is the whole joke: you're set up to expect "so I stopped
    praying" (the standard shape of a lost-faith line) and instead get a kid
    who has PERFECT faith in the mechanics of God, prayer, sin, and
    forgiveness — he just applies the pipeline to a crime instead of a wish.
    No opinion about hypocrisy is ever stated. The joke doesn't say organized
    religion launders bad behavior through forgiveness — it just shows a
    kid using the feature as designed, deadpan, and lets you do the
    indicting. Punch word ("forgive me") is the literal last word of the
    joke, same rule you already have in your template.

    Economy: two sentences, no adjective doing any work, no aside, no
    stated emotion anywhere. This is the "one-sentence setup" rule pushed
    to its floor — the whole joke is barely longer than most people's setup
    alone.


## What to steal (for the card library)

1. **The ratchet escalation** — specificity climbing one real notch at a time until it walks itself off a cliff. You have "escalation" already; the addition here is that the escalation should use a taxonomy that's REAL and checkable, not invented — the audience's own knowledge that this stuff actually exists (real councils, real schisms) is what makes "Me, too!" funny instead of just fast.
2. **Zero-reaction violence/turn** — when the bit's turn is the worst thing in it, give it the SAME punctuation as every line before it. No aside, no "and then, get this—", no stated shock. The flatness sells the ambush.
3. **The fake-deconversion pivot** — set up a sentence that sounds like it's heading toward the audience's expected moral (doubt, disillusionment, growing up) and then have the character's LOGIC stay perfectly intact while only the object of it changes. This is a stronger move than "twist the facts" — it's "twist which direction the belief points, keep the belief total."

Neither joke states a thesis. Both let a straight-faced system (denominational taxonomy; prayer-sin-forgiveness) do 100% of the arguing. That's the throughline to steal, not the religion topic itself.
