## Notes

**VISUAL — the closing frame (must not be skipped).** The entire ending depends on the audience catching **ya → aur**. Spoken fast in Hindi after three near-identical lines, most people miss it. So the last frame carries it: **YA struck through, AUR replacing it.** Pre-censored, text baked into the image prompt (not Canva). This is also the closing frame of the Seedhe Maut reel. Without it the bit stops instead of lands.

**Reel cuts (3):** (1) Tyson — slap → knockout → "I forgive Mike Tyson" → the crown line. (2) The cousin — blocked me / housewarming / rat and King Cobra / dad, dad, wife / crying to mumma. (3) Seedhe Maut lyric on screen as the hook → Dinkar couplet → the YA/AUR frame. Two funny, one save-worthy.

**RAW — second movement, your words, unedited:**
> My cousin who thinks he is right in blocking my number and now he is trying to get in touch with me but i just let him go from my life
>
> it feels nothing 0 but then he goes to my parents then i feel anger because you are 30 year old dont go crying to mumma about it

The turn is in there: **"it feels nothing"** then **anger** three words later. If you'd let him go, the parents thing wouldn't touch you. The anger isn't about him being childish — going to your parents moves the decision to *them*, which takes you out of the seat of the one who grants mercy. He took the crown off (callback to your line 24).

Landing zone: *I've been calling it peace and it was a throne.* Not "I'm pathetic" — the guy with the venom admitting he likes holding it.

Also: `mumma` stays. And `you are 30 year old dont go crying to mumma` is the second laugh in the piece, arriving right on top of the confession — that's the funny protecting the soft part. Don't clean it up.

Parked, not used: parents (he flagged touchy — leave it), friends (a category, not an event).

**What I changed and why:**
- Moved `Now tell me what sounds better` **below** the scene. It was spoiling its own punchline — `after he fucks you up` announced the ending before the scene got to play it. Joke first, argument second.
- Cut `after he fucks you up` — the knockdown shows it now.
- `im fucked up tyson hit me knocked me down` → `Tyson knocks you down`. Three verbs for one event.
- `I / me` → `you`, except inside the quote. You said you don't want it to be about you — this is the fix. The only "I" in the bit is the one he says out loud, which is the one that's funny.
- Kept both `r u nuts` and `r u ok` — they escalate, judgment then concern.

**Live bill (your call, not now):** the bit is a hypothetical, so nobody's exposed yet. If the essay ends without you admitting *you've* done the toothless thing, it becomes a sermon with a Tyson joke bolted on the front. Doesn't have to be a story about you — but something has to pay for the preaching later.

**Parked — live idea, wrong place:** `Well you should have said sorry the minute you slapped Mike Tyson's bald head`. Cut because it gives your own character tactical advice right at the turn into the argument, and it swaps the subject to apology. But the idea under it is real and unused: **sorry is what the toothless have, forgiveness is what the venomous have.** That's its own beat later.

**Cut earlier, parked:**
- `Power and forgivenes -` — a label, not a line
- `Imagine you are` / `of new york` / `you go bezerk and for some reason`
- The four questions — the Hindi already asked all of that
- `Seedhe Maut's song is asking to pick one door` — wrong reading, and "door" was my word, not yours
- `Every second has to be earned / how do we do that` — orphan from something else
- `Let me explain` / `Now these could just be words for you` — throat-clearing
- Of the four questions, the only live one: **"Why are power and forgiveness going together?"** — objects to the *pairing*, which is the real question. The other three objected to the *choosing*. Keep in your pocket.

**Dinkar, corrected and sourced** — *Rashmirathi* (1952), third sarg. Krishna to the Pandavas, arguing them into the war. In the song it's the outro, recited by Dr. Anita Sharma:

> Kshama shobhti us bhujang ko jiske paas garal ho
> Usko kya jo dantheen, vishrahit, vineet, saral ho

(you'd dropped *vishrahit* — venomless — from a couplet about venom)

The stanza right before it is the harder one:

> Atyachar sahan karne ka / kuphal yahi hota hai
> Paurush ka aatank manuj / komal hokar khota hai

**The bridge you haven't spent:** *dantheen* = toothless. Tyson bit a man's ear off. Of everyone alive you picked the one man whose venom is in his mouth. That's how you get from a New York street to a 1952 poem with no seam.

**The title is the structure.** Chorus asks *ya* four times. Title says *aur*. Encore's last line before the outro: *"Do mere shashtra hai shakti aur kshama."* The song refuses its own question on the cover.
