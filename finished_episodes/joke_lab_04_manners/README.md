## THE SIX DECISIONS ON TRIAL

Each one has a number attached. When you analyse, go down this list — don't judge the episode as one blob.

**1. Black-flip thumbnail** *(first one — black-dominant + acid yellow accent, replacing the all-yellow style)*
→ **Judge on CTR, not views.** >2% = the flip earned its place. <1% = revert to yellow. In between = inconclusive, run it again.

**2. Title shortened** — full spine line was 65 chars and truncates at ~60 in feeds, and the cut would have landed on "…with manners", killing it. Shortened to "Let's Hate Each Other With Manners."
→ **Judge on impressions + CTR together.** Impressions moving = the algorithm found people. CTR moving = the title did the work.

**3. Tuesday publish** — deliberately broke the Fri–Sun window. Reasoning at the time: at this scale day-of-week is noise, and holding a finished tape three days invites a re-record spiral.
→ **Judge on day-7 views vs the Fri–Sun uploads.** If it's flat, the publish window is dead as a rule and he stops timing releases.

**4. Runtime 5:29** — he explicitly asked to pad UP from 4 beats/~6 min to 8 min, and it still came in at 5:29 on tape.
→ **Judge on retention %, WITH the caveat that short episodes flatter the percentage.** The honest number is *average view duration in seconds*, not %. Compare seconds to Joke Lab #1–3.

**5. One threaded bit, six beats** *(vs the 2–3 observation format)*
→ **Judge on the retention CURVE, not the average.** Where does it drop? If people leave between beats, threading didn't fix the list problem. If they leave inside a beat, that beat is long.

**6. Reels as whole beats** — a reel here is one entire beat, not a joke run.
→ **Judge on Instagram, never YouTube Shorts** (his Joke Lab reels have never popped on Shorts). Benchmark = **1,600 views**, set by both ep1 reels.

## PROCESS NOTES WORTH KEEPING *(craft, not numbers)*

- The title line came from **him**, unprompted, mid-spar — after five failed Socratic swings on the same beat. The coach's framing (abandonment, the literal death-read on the wife) was rejected twice by him and both times he was right.
- The one Claude-written scaffold in this episode (mom's sign-off) had its ending **replaced** by him — "Prateek, tum bhaad mai jaao" beat the scaffold outright. Scaffolds work as a target to beat, never as a draft to keep.
- Parked, do not re-pitch: the "she might be dead" literal read on the wife; the abandonment / "no logical reason, it's purely emotional" thread.
- Thesis correction he made mid-build: the phone hang-up doesn't actually anger him — it's an observed absurdity. The rage is for PHYSICAL leaving. That gap between calm-about-parents and furious-at-wife IS the self-implication.
