### BEAT 1 — THE DEATH RULE *(locked)*

> There's a ritual in my house. When I leave, I can't say "bye." Saying bye is a crime. And I can't just leave silently either — that's an even bigger crime. I have to say "mai aata hu" — loosely, "I'll see you." No goodbyes in my house. Because in my house, saying bye means bye forever. Dead. The only thing that could kill me is one word — "bye." Now you'd think this doesn't apply on the phone. Everyone says bye on a call, right? Not my parents. For them, bye is still a killer. So what do they do? They just hang up. They're talking, talking — and all of a sudden — silence.

- **BUTTON: "…all of a sudden — silence." NOTHING after it.**
- 🎙️ "house" repeats four times — thin it at the mic.
- 🎙️ "mai aata hu" stays in Hindi. Quick gloss, don't translate it away.
- 🧠 **CONSISTENT, not hypocritical.** They're not rude — they follow the death rule so faithfully that with no phone version of "mai aata hu," vanishing mid-sentence is their only legal exit. Play them *obedient*.
- 🚫 The "she might be dead" angle on the wife was **rejected tonight.** Don't let this beat's death logic tempt you into it in Beat 4.

**[ASIDE — 1 slot]** how insane the superstition sounds when you say it out loud.

### BEAT 3 — MOM'S SIGN-OFF *(locked 10 Aug — his rewrite beat the scaffold)*

>
> She says it to my sister. She says it to the neighbour aunty. She said it to our insurance agent
>
> Me? **"Prateek, tum bhaad mai jaao."**

- **NOTHING after "jaao."** No translation, no gloss. It's a quoted artifact.
- 🎙️ **Say both Hindi lines as HER, not as narration.** Two different voices — the sweet one for the insurance guy, the flat one for you. That contrast is the joke.
- 🎙️ Your **name in her mouth** is the funniest thing in the beat. Don't swallow it.
- 🧠 Why it works: the courtesy isn't missing, it's **allocated.** The further someone is from her, the better the goodbye they get. You get a bespoke curse — which is, technically, still a proper sign-off. She has one for you. It's just that one.
- ✂️ CUT (was in his first pass, stays cut): "always just disconnect or…" (two options dilute the punch) and "This is her favorite line when it comes to me" (explains it).
- 🎙️ Insurance guy is the punch position in the list — if a truer/pettier recipient occurs to you at the mic, swap it in. Smaller is funnier.

### BEAT 5 — THE COMPOUNDING DEBT *(locked 10 Aug)*

> She called me once — I could not pick up. She called me twice — I could not pick up. But now she is calling me every day. And now I *want* to pick up. But I don't pick up. Because I will have to give her the explanation for why I did not pick up the 3 times that she called.
>
> But then, unlike my other friends, she kept calling me. And one day I had to pick up.
>
> **This is how a best friend is made.**

- **TAG:** *A guy would have given up at the first attempt.* — **NOTHING after "first attempt."**
- 🎙️ **FIX THE COUNT AT THE MIC.** "once… twice… every day" then "the 3 times" don't agree. Keep **3**, drop "every day."
- 🎙️ **A system, not a confession.** Sensible math: each missed call raises the price of picking up the next one, until it's cheaper to lose the friend than to explain yourself.
- 🎙️ **"is made" is passive on purpose.** You vanish from your own sentence. Do NOT switch to "this is how I choose."
- ⚠️ She is "she" throughout. No name. Her real message stays off stage.
- ✂️ STAYS CUT: "one of the reasons I ghost my friends" — explains this beat AND spends Beat 6's punch early.

**[ASIDE — 1 slot]** *"ohh that's a bad one"* / *"I sound like a psycho, no?"*
