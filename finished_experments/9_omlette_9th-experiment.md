I cannot make a perfect omelette.

And I want to be very clear — this is not a skill issue.

I mean, it is. But it's also a spiritual problem at this point.

Every morning, before I make one, I watch a YouTube video.
In that youtube video everything is done in 90 seconds. 
His onions are perfectly chopped
his egg crack perfect
His pan is perfectly oiled 
And his omlette slides out perfectly from the pan

I watch it. I nod. I take notes mentally. I think — today is the day. Today I become an omelette person.

I try to do it
I start sweating the moment i step in the kitchen
There is always eggshells in my which i have to take out

I greese the pan. Put my eggs on it 

And then I flip it.

And it breaks.

Not cleanly. Not dramatically. Just... quietly falls apart. Like it was always going to.

It looks like something that gave up mid-sentence.

Every. Single. Time.

And I stand there for a second, spatula in hand, looking at this ruined egg situation, and I think —

okay. Tomorrow.

But the salt thing is what really broke me.

So one day it was too salty. Okay, fine. Next time — less salt. Still too salty. Next time — even less. Still somehow salty. So eventually I just stopped adding salt altogether.

Now it's not salty.

So currently, after months of research and failure, I have successfully figured out how to make a broken, salt-free omelette.

*pause*

I eat it anyway.

And then the next morning — same three eggs. Same pan. Same YouTube video.

Because today might be different.

---

It never is.

But I make it anyway.

Okay so.

I've been getting a lot of bad news lately.

Not like, one bad thing. A streak. A whole era of bad news. Like the universe looked at my vision board and said — cute. No.

I tried entrepreneurship. Didn't work.
Dabbled in AI. Still dabbling.
Now I'm making a podcast talking to a camera alone in my room, which — if you zoom out — is also not going great.

Every one of these things felt like — okay, this is the one. This time I figured out the flip. This time the omelette stays whole.

It didn't.

---

And here's the thing I can't explain.

I still wake up thinking today is going to be different.

Not because I have evidence. I have the opposite of evidence. I have five years of consistent, well-documented evidence that things might not work out.

And still. Alarm goes off. I get up. Eggs come out of the fridge.

Why?

I genuinely don't know.

Some part of my brain just refuses to accept the data. Keeps saying — yeah but what about today though. Have you considered today might be different.

And I'm like — brain. We've talked about this.

And my brain goes — three eggs or two today?

---

I think everyone is doing this.

The friend who sends the follow-up email after no reply. The one who downloads the gym app again on January 1st. The person on a dating app who, after forty terrible dates, swipes right one more time.

All of us. Broken omelettes. Every day.

Not because we've figured something out. Not because we're optimists with good morning routines.

Just because — I don't know. We haven't fully stopped yet.

---

I don't have a conclusion here.

The omelette still breaks. The news is still mostly bad. I'm still watching the same YouTube video like it's going to suddenly teach me something it hasn't in six months.

But I made one this morning.

And I'll probably make one tomorrow.

And somewhere in that — in that completely stubborn, irrational, slightly embarrassing act of trying again —

I think that's hope.

Not the nice kind. Not the poster kind.

The kind that doesn't even know it's hope. The kind that just wakes up and makes eggs.
