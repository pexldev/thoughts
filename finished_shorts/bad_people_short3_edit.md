## 1. Story B plan — "The Asterisk Was Always There"

**Subtext of Story A:** the scam was pre-decided. The VO deliberately never says "they all knew" (that's the episode button — do not spend it in audio). Story B says it silently.

A tiny asterisk stalks every corporate promise in the reel, getting bolder each scene, until the final frame where it's a wall-sized monument. Read the props in order and you get the silent story: *this scam was rehearsed, the number was decided before the meeting ended, the paperwork was armored, and the fine print won.*

| # | Scene | Prop | What it whispers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S3 | Wall poster: **"WE ARE FAMILY\*"** — asterisk tiny | Every promise here has fine print |
| 2 | S4 | Whiteboard: **"RETENTION SCRIPT — v7"**, steps half-erased | They've run this play six times before. Your friend is episode 7 |
| 3 | S5 | HR's notepad, tilted toward camera: **"FINAL: 10%"** | The appraisal number existed *before* the promise did — written during the mute |
| 4 | S7 | Email fine print, barely legible: **"\*at the sole discretion of the company"** | The "in writing" was never protection. (Plants the episode's legalese autopsy without spending it) |
| 5 | S10 | A single **enormous black asterisk** on the wall behind the deadpan mascot | The fine print won. It's all that's left standing |

**Continuity thread (not a counted egg):** the appraisal letter reading **"10%\*"** — pristine in the mascot's hands in S10, crumpled in his fist in S1. On loop, viewers realize both frames are the same moment. The asterisk also touches the "20%\*" in the S6 deal graphic as a bridge beat.

**The loop is the rewatch mechanism.** Every autoloop is a guaranteed second viewing — eggs 2 and 3 (v7 / FINAL: 10%) are the pause-bait that converts loops into freeze-frames and shares ("bro pause at 0:17").
