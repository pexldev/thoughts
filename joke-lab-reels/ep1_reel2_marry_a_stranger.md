## What You Record (in this order)

---

**JOKE 1**

> "I was at Linking Road and a guy asked — 'Aur bhaiyya, kaisa chal raha hai?' I said 'Thik hoon bhaiyya, aap kaise ho?' He immediately said: 'Belt dekhoge? Belt?' My brain now has a fourth entry."

**Notes:**
- The gap between "aap kaise ho?" and "Belt dekhoge belt?" is the joke. Let it snap.
- "My brain now has a fourth entry" — say it like you're updating a spreadsheet. Resigned, not surprised.

---

**JOKE 2**

> "If someone doesn't fall into any of those categories — I call them a USO. [beat] Unidentified Smiling Object."

**Notes:**
- The beat before "Unidentified Smiling Object" is everything. Let the acronym breathe.
- Flat delivery. You're naming a real phenomenon. The absurdity comes from the sincerity.

---

**JOKE 3**

> "I once said 'you look beautiful' to a lady I passed. I watched her eyes go: WTH. [beat] 'Jaa be tharki.' [beat] ...ohh, maybe he's just complimenting me. And then her mouth said — 'thank you.' [beat] That thank-you had to clear three security layers."

**Notes:**
- Play all three eye-stages separately. Don't rush through them — each is a beat.
- "Three security layers" is the stamp. Say it like you're reading a government document.
- This is the identity badge joke. Women watching will share it as "this is literally me." Men will share it as "lmao this is true."

---

**JOKE 4 — THE CLOSER**

> "Indians won't say hi to a stranger."

*[beat — 1 second]*

> "But we'll marry one."

**Notes:**
- That beat between the two sentences is the entire joke. Do not cut it short.
- "Indians won't say hi" — flat statement, no punchline energy yet.
- "But we'll marry one." — land it small. The quieter you say it, the harder it hits.
- After "one." — stop. Hold. End card.
