## What You're Recording

The brain-classifier bit — the long setup pays for the whole run. Joke 1 is an internally escalating three-step structure; Jokes 2 and 3 are tight. The closer ("He looked disappointed") is the hardest because it says everything with nothing — the audience fills in the gap.

This is the strangest of the three reels. It'll split — some viewers won't get there. Doesn't matter. The ones who do will send it.

**Estimated VO runtime: ~28 seconds**
Full reel with hook frame + end card: ~32 seconds

---

## The Hook (on screen while you say it)

> **"This is how my brain classifies oncoming strangers."**

Say it deadpan. No energy. You're reading out a manual.

---

## What You Record (in this order)

---

**JOKE 1 — the classifier (3-step internal escalation)**

> "If it's a guy in his 20s, wearing an I-card, and smiling — it's charity."

*[beat]*

> "If it's a guy in his 30s, wearing an I-card — it's a credit card."

*[beat]*

> "If it's a guy in his 40s, wearing his child —"

*[longer beat]*

> "...his wallet was stolen, his wife is pregnant, his mother had cancer, she died this morning, he just came from Tata Hospital... and all he needs... is ₹380 for a train ticket to go back home."

**Notes:**
- The 20s and 30s lines are fast. Nearly no space between them.
- Then a noticeable gear-shift before "If it's a guy in his 40s, wearing his child." That pause signals the escalation.
- The ₹380 speech: slow it down. Each item in the list lands separately. Don't race to the punchline — the runtime IS the joke.
- "₹380 for a train ticket" — land it like you've heard this exact pitch eight times.

---

**JOKE 2**

> "I met a USO — a girl in her 20s, no I-card, but smiling. My brain panicked. Then she said: 'Hello, don't worry — I'm not selling anything.' My brain relaxed. [beat] Two minutes later, she was asking me to donate my eyes. [beat] She was right. She wasn't selling. Selling means I get something in return."

**Notes:**
- "My brain panicked" / "My brain relaxed" — fast, clinical. You're narrating a process.
- The beat before "Two minutes later" is the entire setup-to-punchline turn. Make it count.
- "Selling means I get something in return." — this is a definition, not a punchline. Flat. Let the logic do the work.

---

**JOKE 3 — THE CLOSER**

> "I saw the same girl again in the evening. Same spot. A guy was asking her — left eye, or right eye? She was explaining that the donation only happens after he's dead."

*[beat — 1.5 seconds]*

> "He looked disappointed."

**Notes:**
- Everything before "He looked disappointed" is just delivery of facts. No jokes yet.
- The beat before "He looked disappointed" is where the audience processes. Don't fill it.
- "He looked disappointed." — say it like you're reading the police report. Zero commentary. That's the joke.
- After "disappointed." — stop. Complete silence. Hold. End card.
