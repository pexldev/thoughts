## OBSERVATION 1

I noticed in India, a smile is never just a smile. It's either someone trying to sell something or a creep.

Cadence: And i have some jokes on it 
## JOKES

 

1. This is how my brain classifies oncoming strangers.
   If it's a guy in his 20s, wearing an I-card, and smiling... it's charity.
   If it's a guy in his 30s, wearing an I-card, and smiling... its a credit card.
   If it's a guy in his 40s, wearing his child... [beat]
   his wallet was stolen, his wife is pregnant, his mother had cancer, she died this morning, he just came from Tata Hospital... and all he needs... is ₹380 for a train ticket to go back home.

2. If someone doesn't fall into any of these categories, I call them a USO. [beat] Unidentified Smiling Object.

3. I met a USO — a girl in her 20s, no I-card, but smiling. My brain panicked. Then she said, "Hello, don't worry — I'm not selling anything." My brain relaxed. [beat] Two minutes later, she was asking me to donate my eyes. [beat] She was right. She wasn't selling. Selling means I get something in return.

4. I saw the same USO again in the evening. Same spot. A guy was asking her — "left eye, or right eye?" — and she was explaining that the donation only happens after he's dead. [beat] He looked disappointed.

→ *"And this database updates itself, by the way."*

5. I was at Linking Road and a guy asked, "Aur bhaiyya, kaisa chal raha hai?" I said, "Thik hoon bhaiyya, aap kaise ho?" He immediately said: "Belt dekhoge? Belt?" [beat] My brain now has a fourth entry.

*(Run ends. Reset.)*

→ *"You know what all this scanning has done to us?"*

6. Indians won't say hi to a stranger. [beat] But we'll marry one.

→ *"And it's a very selective fear."*

7. If a white man asks me "how's it going?" — I say "good, you?" and move on. If an Indian man asks me "kaise ho?" — I ask him, "aap kaun, bhaisahab?"

→ *"God forbid you go one step past hi."*

8. I once complimented a random lady "you look beautiful" . I watched her eyes go: from WTH. [beat] to "Jaa be tharki." [beat] to ...ohh Maybe he's just complimenting me. And then her mouth said, "thank you." [beat] That thank-you had to clear three security layers.

→ *"Which is funny, because on paper we're the chillest civilization in history."*

9. We taught the world "karm karo, fal ki chinta mat karo." Then someone says hi to us... and we immediately start calculating the fal.

→ *"And before you think I'm above all this—"*

10. I can confidently ask for referrals on LinkedIn. But I get nervous if they say the interview is face-to-face. [beat] I'm a brave and confident man... as long as there's a screen between us.

Our default setting isn't friendliness. It's risk assessment. Scam? Insurance? Donation? MLM? Political campaign? Creep? [beat] Eye donation? [beat] Only after eliminating all seven do we consider... "maybe he's just being nice."

## OBSERVATION 2

For some reason, dustbins are invisible to people in this country.

Cadence: And i have some jokes on it 


## JOKES

1. The rest of the world has bins for wet waste, dry waste, medical waste, e-waste — and we have Blinkit ka thaili. [beat] Our bins are use and throw.

→ *"And when we do find an actual dustbin—"*

2. In this country there's more garbage outside the dustbin than inside it. This is what happens when an entire country was told "you will get 2 marks just for the steps."

→ *"To be fair, some of us are trying."*

3. I once saw a guy in the park aiming a chocolate wrapper into the penguin dustbin's mouth. He missed. I told him "It missed." He said "Yeah I know — that penguin moved."

→ *"And this is not an education problem, by the way."*

4. I saw a guy in the airport bathroom wash his hands, wipe them with a tissue, and throw it in the sink. He had an Apple Watch, a Gabit ring... and somehow the dustbin was the smartest thing in the room.

→ *"It gets worse at the international level."*

5. I saw another guy wearing wayfarers inside Mumbai Airport, throwing trash next to the bin. I realised he was blind — I felt sorry for him, picked his trash and put it in the bin. Then I saw this man in Singapore, just outside immigration, throwing some papers INTO the bin... didn't know Singapore immigration also offers LASIK.

→ *"Honestly, I feel bad for Indian dustbins."*

6. In India dustbins have so much free time, they're doing an MBA. [beat] One of them works part-time at Accenture. [beat] Even the dustbin complains — "yeh kya kachra kaam hai yaar."

→ *"Because we don't throw things in this country — we reuse them."*

7. My friend packed his stool sample in a Zomato container. [beat] That container has seen some shit.

→ *"And that mindset? Inherited."*

8. My dad throws  squeezed lemon out of the window — "the birds will eat it." With that logic, if I throw my Form 16 out, will some CA pick it up and do my taxes?

→ *"And the big items have a different system entirely."*

9. I have a broken chair — I don't know where to throw it, so I dry my clothes on it. In India, furniture never retires. It gets transferred to the balcony. 

→ *"And before you judge all these people—"*

10. I broke up with my ex because she was toxic. I'm still in touch with her. Like my countrymen... I don't know how to dump trash.

→ *"I learned all this young, by the way."*

11. When I was eight, I threw a banana peel on the floor. Five minutes later it was gone. My mom said, "The vacuum took it." Ten years later I realized... we never owned a vacuum cleaner. [beat] But I know a person who helps us in our house for the last 10 years. Maybe she is a house help.

→ *"One last story. This is where it all ends up."*

12. Some guy spat and threw a gutka wrapper into a nalla. People saw him and... they all started copying him. The wrappers clogged the nalla. Then it rained. My house flooded. My furniture got ruined. My mattress got ruined. So I threw everything into the same nalla. [beat] Those wrappers don't clog the nalla anymore.
