## THE SHAPE — three bits, three ENGINES *(settled 17 Aug)*

Not "observation, then jokes." That format died in ep2 and you're the one who called it: *asides fixed the flatness between jokes, they didn't fix that each joke starts from zero.* Ep5 went all the way to one continuous story.

Ep6 sits between the two on purpose. Three bits, and the variety is in the **mechanism**, not the topic:

| Bit | Engine | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Reels | **analogy** | premise → analogy → the menu name IS the turn → 2 tags |
| 2 · Labrador | **story** | setup → the flood → the quoted line → self-turn |
| 3 · Dad / God | **anaphora run ×2** | one sentence stem repeated, twice, callback across the seam |

A repeated stem is ONE joke mechanism, not a list — so §3 is threaded by construction. It's only allowed to be list-shaped because it's the third engine rather than the only one.

### Two rules that follow from this

1. **Nothing on this page gets numbered as "Joke 1, Joke 2."** The numbered page turns into a numbered mouth. Sections are written as continuous beats and read straight down. The only numbered slot in this file is the §3 god run, and that's *inventory ordering* — by record night it's prose.

2. **Every bit ends on a button.** Not a fade, not a "so yeah" — a hard last word with nothing after it. Non-negotiable for a reason beyond taste: **the button is where a reel ends.** A fully-stitched 11-minute narrative gives you one cuttable reel (that was ep5's real cost). Three bits with three buttons give you three doors.

A thesis is the audience's *prediction*. Its whole job is to aim the jokes and tell you what to cut. If a line is funny but sits outside the thesis, it isn't dead — it belongs to a different file.
