# JOKE LAB — LAB NOTES
*(What the last recording taught us. Read before writing/recording the next episode.)*

---

## Episode 1 (recorded & uploaded Jul 10, 2026)

### NOTE 1 — Delivery reads as a list, not a person

**The problem:** The bam-bam run came out sounding like enlisting items — flat, even-paced, lullaby energy. Joke, joke, joke with no human in between. The format is rapid-fire, but rapid-fire ≠ monotone.

**The fix:** Break character between jokes. Add informal asides — real reactions, like talking to a friend, not reading a manual:

- *"Ohh that is such a bad one."*
- *"Idk why I like this one — let me know if you did."*
- *"My dad always does this."*
- *"If my dad hears this he will kill me."*

**How to apply:**
- These asides are the *connective tissue* between jokes — 1–2 seconds each, thrown away, not performed.
- Not after every joke — that becomes its own pattern. Sprinkle 3–4 per observation, especially after the jokes that are weird, personal, or risky.
- Recording scripts should WRITE THESE IN from now on (with room to improvise better ones in the moment). A script with zero asides is a script that will record flat.
- The asides double as the "lab" identity: reacting to your own jokes IS the format — you're testing them out loud.

## Episode 2 (Jul 2026)

### NOTE 2 — Listing jokes still reads unnatural; build BITS, not lists

**The problem:** Even with asides in, an observation delivered as "joke, joke, joke" still feels like reading a numbered list. Asides fixed the flatness between jokes; they didn't fix that each joke starts from zero — the audience gets re-recruited every 15 seconds instead of riding one continuous thought.

**The fix (format change, locked Jul 18):** Each observation is now built as a threaded **bit** — a run of jokes that ride one shared opinion, not a list that shares a topic.

**How to apply:**
- The glue is the shared OPINION, not shared setups or long segues. If every joke is another angle on the same charge, bridges can be one breath: "and the other thing about this line...", "even the dogs know this..."
- Jokes ordered to escalate; a tag on one joke can BE the setup of the next.
- Plant one callback early in the run that detonates at the end.
- Bridges are voice — he writes them in his own mouth-words at assembly. Claude threads the order/structure only.
- Asides from Note 1 stay in — they're the mortar inside the bit, same rules as before.
- Prep files don't change (jokes are still the atoms); the threading happens at episode assembly.
