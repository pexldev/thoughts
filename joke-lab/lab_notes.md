## Episode 1 (recorded & uploaded Jul 10, 2026)

### NOTE 1 — Delivery reads as a list, not a person

**The problem:** The bam-bam run came out sounding like enlisting items — flat, even-paced, lullaby energy. Joke, joke, joke with no human in between. The format is rapid-fire, but rapid-fire ≠ monotone.

**The fix:** Break character between jokes. Add informal asides — real reactions, like talking to a friend, not reading a manual:

- *"Ohh that is such a bad one."*
- *"Idk why I like this one — let me know if you did."*
- *"My dad always does this."*
- *"If my dad hears this he will kill me."*

**How to apply:**
- These asides are the *connective tissue* between jokes — 1–2 seconds each, thrown away, not performed.
- Not after every joke — that becomes its own pattern. Sprinkle 3–4 per observation, especially after the jokes that are weird, personal, or risky.
- Recording scripts should WRITE THESE IN from now on (with room to improvise better ones in the moment). A script with zero asides is a script that will record flat.
- The asides double as the "lab" identity: reacting to your own jokes IS the format — you're testing them out loud.

## Episode 2 (Jul 2026)

### NOTE 2 — Listing jokes still reads unnatural; build BITS, not lists

**The problem:** Even with asides in, an observation delivered as "joke, joke, joke" still feels like reading a numbered list. Asides fixed the flatness between jokes; they didn't fix that each joke starts from zero — the audience gets re-recruited every 15 seconds instead of riding one continuous thought.

**The fix (format change, locked Jul 18):** Each observation is now built as a threaded **bit** — a run of jokes that ride one shared opinion, not a list that shares a topic.

**How to apply:**
- The glue is the shared OPINION, not shared setups or long segues. If every joke is another angle on the same charge, bridges can be one breath: "and the other thing about this line...", "even the dogs know this..."
- Jokes ordered to escalate; a tag on one joke can BE the setup of the next.
- Plant one callback early in the run that detonates at the end.
- Bridges are voice — he writes them in his own mouth-words at assembly. Claude threads the order/structure only.
- Asides from Note 1 stay in — they're the mortar inside the bit, same rules as before.
- Prep files don't change (jokes are still the atoms); the threading happens at episode assembly.

## Episode — "Nobody Watches The Demo" (recorded 17 Sep 2026)
*First episode under the locked format ([ops/the_format.md](../ops/the_format.md)). ~3 min. Both notes below were found by HIM, listening back — the first time the gap was named without prompting.*

### NOTE 3 — No tags. Every joke lands once and exits.

**The problem:** eight punches, eight immediate exits. Dolphin → next. *Copying my answers* → next. *Including your children* → next. The script is correct, tight, and slightly airless — and the sensation it produces is *"something's missing and I can't point at it."* That's what a tagless script feels like: tags are invisible when present, and their absence just reads as the thing moving too fast.

**The fix (already in his own prep template, never run):**
> *2 tags per joke — stay in the joke's world one beat longer, smaller and stranger. A tag RE-FRAMES; it never explains.*

Mitch's escalator is the reference: *"escalator temporarily stairs"* is the joke, *"sorry for the convenience"* is the tag — and the tag is the bigger laugh.

**How to apply:** it's the cheapest move in comedy — no new ideas needed, you stay inside a joke you already have for one more beat. Run the Tag Pass on the keepers **before** assembly, not after. Target 2–3 tagged jokes per episode, not all of them.

### NOTE 4 — Segues missing at the topic-joins.

**The problem:** two cold starts in the middle of the episode —
- styles → the recorded-voice/AI section
- AI → *"I always prefer the emergency exit seats"* ← **the biggest gap in the piece.** Complete stop, fresh start.

**What a segue actually is here — the part that matters:** in a threaded bit, **a segue is not a linking phrase. It's the argument continuing.** Those two joins feel cold because the sections are stitched by TOPIC (everything happens on a plane) instead of by the thing being argued. A topic-join always reads as a new paragraph, however smooth the sentence is. Glue = the shared opinion (Note 2, 18 Jul). A bridge is one breath: *"and the other thing about this…"*

**Not everywhere, though.** The episode has three joins that work and must not be "fixed":
- *"Now it's not because I'm a pervert — it's because…"* — antithesis, carries the argument forward
- *"But sometimes I think about the cabin crew"* — marks the gear change into the quiet section
- **The join into the close is abrupt ON PURPOSE.** *She's bombing.* `[beat]` *I come here every week and bomb.* No bridge — the abruptness IS the joke. Deliberate, keep.

### What worked — don't lose these next time
- **Two callbacks built by ear, neither announced:** *the entire flight* (her fantasy) → *the entire flight* (hooked to the baby) → *the entire internet.* And *pay attention* changing owner four times, ending in a crew member shouting it at a passenger.
- **The reversal spine:** opens as the only one watching an ignored performer, closes as the ignored performer. Never stated out loud.
- **One act-out** (*"Sir. Sir. SIR."*) — the first in any episode, and the liveliest thing on the tape.
- **Asides deliberately dropped, correctly.** Note 1 was diagnosed on a joke LIST; this is one continuous piece with nothing to bridge between, and breaking character would have deflated the mirror section. **Asides fix lists, not threaded bits.**
