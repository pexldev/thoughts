# Topic: EXPLAIN IT TO ME LIKE I'M A LABRADOR

Built during the 13 Aug spar, then pulled out of the shooting-in-the-dark episode — that episode went single-observation (the Tony story) and cutting to a corporate meeting would have broken the world. This beat is finished enough to drop into any episode about faking competence.

---

## The draft (his words, setup already de-flinched)

> When I was about three years into my career, I was working on this project where, out of the entire team, I was the only one who understood the ins and outs of it.
>
> One day, I had to present it to senior leadership. And obviously, I wanted to show off.
>
> So I threw in every jargon I'd learned in those three years.
>
> Halfway through, this guy from senior leadership stops me and says:
>
> "Explain it to me like I'm a Labrador."

---

## Facts established in the spar — keep them straight

- He **could** explain it plainly. He did not fail. The earlier "I struggled" was a flinch and he walked it back himself.
- The plain version, said live, one sentence, zero jargon:
  > "We are trying to make a system that will help users track everything related to what happens before, after and during the sale of your product."
- Why he didn't open with that: **"show off."** His word.
- So the shame isn't incompetence — it's that he could speak plainly the whole time and chose the costume.
- Cut in session: *"or at least I thought I did"* after the setup. It warned the audience the turn was coming and left the Labrador line nothing to break. Don't put it back.

## ⭐ The self-turn — the find

Asked for the actual jargon he threw, he typed:

> "Its like FSL and BSA and TDD and LAV and TRV **any random abbrevations**"

He can't remember what his own jargon meant. Three years of vocabulary, aimed at a man who could fire him, and today it's "any random abbreviations."

**Mechanism to build:** rule of three / escalation flood on the abbreviations, said fast and straight, then the small true landing. He does not need the real ones — client-specific terms are off-limits anyway, and it's funnier when they're obviously nothing.

## Unbuilt

- What he was scared the room would think if he spoke plainly. Asked three times, never landed — stop asking directly. Come at it sideways (give it a voice, or make him act out the plain version and watch where he flinches).
- ⚠️ Never name the actual firm. Swap to KPMG/PwC/EY/Accenture.
