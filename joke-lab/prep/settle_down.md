# settle down

**Vein:** sayings taken apart
**Premise:** "Settle" is a physics word — it's what sediment does. It got smuggled into a sentence about a life. Nothing leaves the glass; the dirt just moves to where nobody looks, and the top becomes presentable.
**Mechanism:** sense pun / literal break — plant the literal meaning FIRST as flat fact, then let the phrase land on it. The audience makes the connection; that connection is the laugh.

---

## KEEPER — 23 Aug 2026 (spar)

> A dirty glass of water. The impurities have settled down.
> "Mera beta settle ho gaya hai," says that uncle.
> The glass looks clean.

**His cut, and it's the right one** — the restating line ("the impurities have settled") is gone. The bit never says the son IS the impurity; the audience says it. Bigger leap = bigger laugh.

Rules that made it work: glass planted before uncle appears, no "imagine / now / it means / you know" (all stage directions), no simile — never say "it's like that glass". Hindi survives only as uncle's quoted line. Punch lands on "clean".
