## OPEN — ✅ WRITTEN 14 Aug

### The room

Sometimes when I am working from home and there is no one at home.

There is this silence.

That silence is only an external silence.

Internally there are 10 versions of me arguing over 100 life choices I made in past 6 months.

So I play some random podcast.

I make the room around me louder to silence the voice inside me.

### The drop

2 comics — American — on top of their game — doing this for the past 10 years — they say we all are shooting in the dark.

No one knows what they are doing.

All of this Dan Soder said to Chris DiStefano in a podcast being recorded in the back of his car, in the middle of the street of Manhattan.

### The confession

Now if you have listened to any episodes of this podcast, you know I crib about no one watching, no subscribers, not even my friends.

But when I hear that the top comics in the world are also struggling through this — and even they are just shooting in the dark, like I am with this podcast —

For some reason, when I see someone who is better than me but has the same problem as me, it makes me feel better.

Fuck the work. Let's listen to what these 2 legends have to say.

---

### Rules on the open

- **Ends on "legends."** He calls them legends in the same breath they admit they're clueless — that contradiction is the joke. Don't tag it, don't explain it.
- **Two beats, not one:** "To make the room around you louder. To silence the voice inside you." Take the pause between them; the pause sells the contradiction.
- **"Not even my friends" ends the list. Full stop.** He decided 14 Aug to cut the follow-up about them being the ones who asked him to start. Rule of three works because items 1 and 2 are numbers and item 3 is people.
- Names out loud: **Soder**, **DiStefano**.
- Watch: "working from home and there is no one at home" — *home* twice in one line. His call in the read-through.

### Cut from the open — do not restore

- *"Today for example there is nothing coming to my head…"* — the whole original placeholder. "For example" was an orphan pointing at a thought the audience never heard, and there was no laugh in the first thirty seconds.
- *"And that random podcast there will be a random line which sticks with you / now that is what happened to me"* — announced the joke instead of telling it.
- *"on Youtube"* — so the line lands on **podcast**.
- *"I do not have any logical explanation to this"* and *"made me feel relieved"* — stated emotion, replaced by "fuck the work."
- *"sadistic pleasure" / "sharing people's misery"* — labels. Terms don't get laughs; the behaviour does.

### Placement question for the read-through

"Fuck the work, let's listen to what these 2 legends have to say" is chronologically the moment he heard it — so it *could* sit right after the Manhattan line. It's parked at the end of the confession instead, because it's the punch and punches go last. Try it both ways at the mic.

## BEAT 4 — Tony arrives

And then comes Tony.

Blue eyed. Tall but lanky. Comes to college in an SUV.

He dresses good. smells good and has a cool backpack.

You see, in college these are the things you can show off. A backpack. He had a cool backpack. And I used to think *that* is why he is cool. Not because he had a rich father, or because his name was Tony in an Indian college — but because he had a cool backpack.

A backpack which was different from everyone's. In college we all had the same backpack — 2 zips, one for your laptop and the other for your books and stationery. But he had that backpack that looked like a sack with ropes to tie it. No zip.

That was so cool.

And he was a good friend of mine. One of my best friends. Because him and I, we both were Malayalees. And 2 Malayalees can either be best friends or arch rivals. There is nothing in between.

And one day it's pouring rain.

*(pause — TAKE the full two seconds. Do not fill it.)*

And then I said: "Congratulations guys. I'm so happy for you."

### Rules on this beat

- **Ends on "I'm so happy for you."** Nothing after it. Don't tag it, don't explain it, don't add "Tony is a madarchod" here — that word is reserved for the final close and it only detonates once.
- **CUT:** *"it felt like 2 hours but I think it was only 2 seconds."* Don't describe the pause, take it. Explaining silence is you covering for silence.
- **CUT:** *"it broke me"* and *"sad from inside, happy from outside."* Both are stated emotion — his oldest tell, and it always arrives exactly at the embarrassing part. The behaviour already carries it: he asked her, then congratulated them.
- **"Guys"** is load-bearing. He's already merged them into a unit in the same breath he loses her.
- The invitation is the joke: he wasn't collateral damage, he was **driven to the viewing.**
