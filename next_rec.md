There is  a couple who lives one floor below me.

If my flat number is 1001...
hers is 901.

And almost every weekend...

they fight.

Not the normal husband-wife argument where you hear a few loud voices and then silence.

This is different.

A few minutes later...

she goes into the bathroom attached to their master bedroom...

and cries.

Not crying.

Weeping.

The kind of crying where even if you don't know the person...
you immediately know something is terribly wrong.

Now...
I don't know these people.

We've never spoken.

But when you live in an apartment...
you accidentally become part of other people's lives.

From what I've overheard over the last few months...

I think her husband cheated on her.

I think she caught him.

I think he even admitted it.

And from one of those fights...
I think he asked for a divorce.

I could be completely wrong.

I'm only hearing one apartment through concrete walls.

Maybe I've stitched together an entire Netflix series from random sentences.

But one thing I know for sure...

she cries.

Almost every weekend.

Sometimes late into the night she's calling him.

He isn't picking up.

Or he isn't coming home.

I don't know.

And every single time...

I have the same thought.

Should I just go downstairs...

knock on the door...

and ask,

"Hey... are you okay?"

"Do you want to come upstairs?"

"I've got friends over almost every weekend."

"Just sit with us for a while."

Because from whatever little I've seen...

I don't think she has many people around.

She seems...

alone.

And then immediately another thought comes.

What would she think?

Imagine some random guy from upstairs...

who somehow knows details about your marriage...

knocking on your door.

That sounds less like kindness...

and more like the beginning of a crime documentary.

So I never go.

Our house help knows her.

She always says,

"Didi bahut achi hai...

bhaiyya bewda hai."

Maybe she's right.

Maybe she isn't.

Because that's the other thing I've realised.

You never know the whole story.

I'm listening through walls.

My house help hears one version.

His friends probably have another version.

He probably has one too.

Nobody wakes up thinking,

"You know what?

Today I'll become the villain in someone else's life."

People always have a story that makes sense to them.

Still...

the way she cries...

it's difficult not to feel something.

And then I think...

if your relationship has reached this point...

why stay?

Why do people hold on so tightly?

Marriage.

Boyfriend.

Girlfriend.

Whatever.

If every weekend has become another episode of emotional destruction...

why not just leave?

Even if divorce is complicated...

live separately.

Figure it out.

At least stop hurting each other.

Maybe it's fear.

Fear of being alone.

Fear of what society will say.

Fear of starting over.

Maybe the relationship has become familiar...

even if it's miserable.

I don't know.

I've never understood why some people find it impossible to leave.

Maybe if I were in that situation...

I'd behave exactly the same way.

Who knows?

All I know is...

every weekend...

I'm sitting in my room working...

and through the floor...

I can hear someone else's marriage falling apart.

And it's such a strange feeling.

To witness someone's pain...

without them even knowing you're witnessing it.

You want to help.

But maybe helping would make everything worse.

So you do the only thing you can do.

Nothing.

And somehow...

doing nothing...

feels awful too.

look at the irony of it all 
There is a lady in my building about whom i know a lot 
but she does not know that i know 
and im here recording an entire podcast about it
and she is there thinking how lonely she is
