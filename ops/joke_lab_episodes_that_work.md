# What Makes a Joke Lab Friday Work

The volume-format companion to `reels_that_work.md` and `wednesday_episodes_that_work.md`. Joke Lab plays a different game than both: the Wednesday episode is a *show*, Joke Lab is a **factory with a public showroom**. Judge it like a factory — by throughput, cost, and what ships out of it — never by the showroom's foot traffic.

---

## 1. What Joke Lab is for

The format's own definition (set July 2026): the long-form Friday episode is the **archive**; the Shorts cut from it are the **product**. Everything follows from that:

1. **Widen the funnel.** Nobody — not us, not anyone — can predict which joke travels. The honest response to that is volume: ~10 jokes per observation, 2–3 observations a week, ~1,500 attempts a year. The hit rate doesn't need to be good; the denominator needs to be big.
2. **Buy reps.** Volume is deliberate practice in public. The 400th "take it literally" joke is written faster and lands harder than the 40th. Joke Lab is how the Wednesday episodes quietly get funnier.
3. **Feed the machine.** Each Friday should yield 1–2 reels-worthy runs (the actual product) and occasionally an observation strong enough to graduate into a full Wednesday bit. The lab feeds the show.
4. **Build an appointment.** Same day, same anatomy, same branding, every week. Format recognition trains return viewers faster than quality does — people follow *formats* ("Friday = Joke Lab") before they follow creators.

**The #1 rule of the format: cadence beats quality.** A mediocre Joke Lab that ships Friday is a success; a brilliant one that slips to Sunday is a failure. Some episodes will be weak — that's not a bug, that's the lab's premise, and the audience is in on it.

---

## 2. Why volume formats win in the real world

This is the open-mic model, made public. Every working comedian builds an hour by testing hundreds of jokes in small rooms and keeping the survivors — the special is the showroom, the open mics are the factory. Joke Lab collapses that: the open mic IS the show.

What that buys, concretely:

- **The audience becomes the A&R department.** Comments, rewatches, and which cuts travel tell us which jokes deserve the Wednesday treatment — data no amount of self-editing produces.
- **Process is a genre.** "Building in public" has its own audience: people who enjoy watching *how* the thing gets made, and who root for the maker. They're fewer than joke-enjoyers but they convert to subscribers at a much higher rate — watching someone's process creates investment.
- **Failure is content.** A joke bombing in a lab is honest and watchable; the same joke bombing in a polished special is just bad. The lab frame converts our duds from liabilities into texture — and makes the hits feel *earned* rather than manufactured.
- **Low stakes protect the main brand.** Experiments happen on Friday so Wednesday never has to gamble.

## 3. What makes a Joke Lab episode good anyway

Volume format ≠ careless format. The quality gate moves to the reels stage, but the episode still has craft — it's just *rhythm* craft, not polish craft:

**a) Cadence is the product.** Observation stated cold → bam bam bam bam → next. Zero connective tissue, zero explaining. The moment a joke gets explained, the format's promise breaks. (House rule: don't explain too early — podcast_rules.md.)

**b) Runs must escalate.** Within each observation, order the jokes so they climb — and the count creates its own suspense ("joke 7/10" is a progress bar; people stay to see the set complete). Three same-level jokes in a row reads as a plateau even in a lab.

**c) The lineup needs contrast.** Three observations of the same species blur together. Mix types: a cliché (grass is greener) + a person (cargo-pants guy) + a what-if (AI panic attacks) beats three what-ifs. Order like a set: second-strongest opens, weakest hides in the middle, strongest closes.

**d) Duds stay in.** Sanitizing the lab kills the premise. A couple of visible misses per episode are load-bearing: they prove the hits are real, they keep the "lab" frame honest, and they give the audience their job —

**e) The post-credit is the community engine.** "Jokes that didn't make the cut — you decide" flips viewers from audience into judges. Judges comment. Comments are free distribution and the strongest early signal of which material deserves a reel. Never ship the segment empty.

**f) It costs 30 minutes to edit. Hard cap.** A handful of images, reused frames, text stamps carrying the variation. The cheapness isn't a compromise — it's what makes weekly shipping survivable, and the scrappy look *is* the lab's visual honesty.

## 4. The weekly loop

1. **Through the week:** observations land in `joke-lab/prep/` via the ladder (`/joke-lab-content`) — capture fast, judge never.
2. **Assemble:** 2–3 prep files → episode script. Ordering per section 3c.
3. **Ship Friday.** Edit via `/joke-lab-editor`, 30 minutes, done. Shipped beats better.
4. **Harvest:** score each observation's best run on the Virality Quotient scorecard (`reels_that_work.md`), cut the top 1–2 as reels via `/joke-lab-reels`. The reels point back to the episode and to Wednesday.
5. **Listen:** which jokes got quoted in comments, which cut traveled, which observation died silently.
6. **Graduate:** an observation that overperforms earns the full treatment — expanded into a Wednesday bit via `/content`. The lab's biggest wins don't stay in the lab.

## 5. What to measure — the factory's dashboard

Joke Lab hasn't shipped yet (Episode 1 in progress, July 2026), so we set the KPIs before vanity metrics can volunteer themselves. In order:

1. **The streak.** Consecutive Fridays shipped. This is the metric — a broken streak is the only real failure the format has.
2. **Edit time.** ≤30 minutes. If it's creeping, the *system* is broken (too many images, too much polish), not the effort.
3. **Harvest yield.** Reels-worthy runs per episode (VQ score 7+). Target: 1–2. This is the factory's actual output.
4. **Comment rate on the post-credit segment.** Is the "you decide" loop working?
5. **Graduation rate.** Observations promoted to Wednesday material per month. Even 1 is compounding.

Explicitly NOT a KPI: **episode view count.** The archive gets discovered backwards when a reel pops — until then, 20 views on a Friday episode is the expected, healthy cost of running the factory.

## 6. The Lab scorecard — ship-day test

Run this Friday morning, not during the week (pre-judging violates the volume rule):

| # | Question | Points |
|---|----------|--------|
| 1 | **Shipping today?** No slippage, no "one more pass"? | /2 |
| 2 | **Cold entry:** first observation stated inside 10 seconds — no format re-explanation after episode 1? | /1 |
| 3 | **Volume honest:** ~10 jokes per observation on the page, duds included? | /1 |
| 4 | **Runs escalate:** each observation's jokes ordered to climb, hardest last? | /1 |
| 5 | **Lineup contrast:** observation types mixed (cliché / person / what-if), strongest closing? | /1 |
| 6 | **Harvest visible:** at least one run you'd already bet on as a reel? | /1 |
| 7 | **Post-credit loaded:** 2–4 cut jokes + the "you decide" ask? | /1 |
| 8 | **Budget held:** edit plan ≤9 new images, ≤30 minutes? | /1 |
| 9 | **Runtime:** ~15 minutes, not ballooning past 18? | /1 |

**8–10:** ship it.
**5–7:** ship it anyway — fix the *system* next week (the scorecard exists to improve episode N+1, never to delay episode N).
**≤4:** still ship something — even two observations instead of three. The streak survives everything except not shipping.

## 7. Anti-patterns — what kills a volume format

- **Over-polishing.** The deadliest one. Every hour spent making a Joke Lab "better" is stolen from next week's episode and teaches the format to slip. If it's tempting to skip a Friday to raise quality, the format is already dying.
- **Explaining the joke.** Adding context before or after a punchline breaks the bam-bam contract. If a joke needs setup beyond the observation, it belongs on Wednesday, not in the lab.
- **Topics instead of observations.** "AI" is a topic; "what if AI has panic attacks" is an observation. Topics produce essays; observations produce jokes. If the prep-file premise has no *angle* in it, it isn't ready.
- **Judging while writing.** Trimming to the best 5 during the week is the reels skill's job on Saturday, not the writer's job on Tuesday. Volume first, gate later.
- **KPI confusion.** Panicking about Friday view counts leads to polishing, which breaks cadence, which kills the actual product line. Read section 5 again before reading the analytics report.
- **Edit creep.** Scene lists growing, one-off images multiplying, a second expression kit sneaking in. The 30-minute cap is a design constraint, not a suggestion — when the edit grows, cut images, never add time.
- **Sanitizing.** Shipping only bangers makes Joke Lab a worse Wednesday episode instead of a good lab. The misses are part of the promise.
