# Best comebacks — banked from sparring

## Keepers (mine, tested) - Jul 9

**"You've changed."**
→ "Yes, one of us has to."
(re-aimed the 'getting old' shell — proof the shells transfer)

**"You're not funny."**
→ "I know — I uploaded 30 jokes on YouTube. 1 view, 1 like. Both were mine."
(agree + escalate with numbers, punch word last)

**"You're not funny."** (alt shell)
→ "I know — my wife tells me every morning."
(outsource the insult to a crueler heckler)

**"How are you still single?"**
→ "On the 2nd date she caught me cheating with Claude."
(misdirection — "cheating" resolves wrong. GRADUATED TO MATERIAL: tag = she reads the chat: "She just ate 3 onions, the food is yet to arrive." Claude: "Her mouth will stink for 3 days." → take to joke lab)

**"When are you getting a real job?"**
→ "You're right, this isn't one — real jobs pay you."
(agree with the loaded word "real," break it on the true gap. Parked it mid-session, arrived 10 minutes later — parking works)

## Keepers — session 3, roast mode (Jul 11)

**Friend 45 min late: "bro, Mumbai traffic."**
→ "Yeah, traffic. Your breath says you woke up half an hour ago. You weren't stuck in traffic — you were stuck between a mattress and a blanket."
(full run: flat agree → evidence testifies a timestamp → loaded-word flip on "stuck." Hallway version = last line alone; the breath beat is the set version)

**Friend shows off iPhone 17 Pro Max on 24-month EMI.**
→ "Congratulations brother, you're going to get such a good RoI on that. In 24 months exactly 2 phones would be better than that depreciating asset."
(backhanded compliment: sincere praise in HIS consultant vocabulary, stab hidden in the reason. "Exactly" = the forensic-precision signature move again. Punch word last)

**Friend orders triple cheeseburger + large fries + Diet Coke: "trying to be healthy these days."**
→ "Yes — that Diet Coke has a negative calorific value. It subtracts the 2000 calories you gain from that one meal."
(sarcastic over-agreement: accept his premise as true, extend the logic with spec-sheet confidence. "Negative calorific value" = engineer voice delivering absurdity as fact)

**Friend wearing sunglasses indoors at a house party, 9 PM.**
→ "I've seen US Marines wearing those exact glasses. They wear them to wars. You wear them to walls."
(comparison roast: gear vs mission mismatch + one-letter sound flip wars→walls. The image is TRUE — his whole night is standing at a wall. "Those exact glasses" = forensic signature. Arrived via the blank-fill drill: list 5 boring answers fast, the kill was hiding in the list)

**Friend debuts a terrible haircut: "how's the new look?"**
→ "Brother, are you okay? Whoever did that to you clearly does not like you."
(fake sympathy: haircut reframed as assault — check on the victim, then infer the attacker's motive. The insult stays SILENT; the worry implies everything. Understatement "does not like you" keeps the mask on better than "hates you" would)

**Rules from session 3 (roast mode):**
- A roast is an observation with teeth: the exaggeration is fake, the direction is TRUE.
- Backhanded compliment: aim the praise one inch off-target — the *reason* for the praise is the wound.
- Over-agreement law: NEVER switch sides. The insult arrives through the logic; your face stays on his team. (Mask slips that died: "5 meals," "the plate," "cardiac arrest.")
- Cartoon-big numbers say "I'm joking." Audit-precise numbers say "I believe this." Precision is the funnier lie.
- Comparison trap: comparing him to someone cool = a compliment. The roast lives in the gear-vs-mission MISMATCH.
- Fake sympathy law: never say the quiet part ("insane," "therapist"). The concern implies it — naming it is just an insult with extra steps.
- When stuck finding a comparison: you named the trait too narrowly. Re-name it by FUNCTION, then walk who does it for real.
- The truth filter is internal now: gf-cheating inventions got self-rejected mid-swing ("but all this is not true") — that's the session-1 law running on autopilot.

## Keepers — session 5, mask discipline (Jul 12)

**Gym-bro cousin, flexing: "Bro you should train with me. I could fix that body in 3 months."**
→ "This body can only be FIXED by god in 3 months, but for that I will have to go to him."
(over-agreement + escalation: the contractor upgrades from cousin to God, "3 months" quote kept intact, punch lands on "him" — audience does the funeral math alone. Mask never slips. Bonus rep: he self-cut the explaining tail "he is not coming to me" before the coach could — the explanation-check editor is going internal)

**Colleague promoted over you, hand on shoulder: "No hard feelings bro. Hard work pays off — you should try it sometime."**
→ "Thank you, totally can't agree more. I need to learn the way you deal with clients & how you get your way in smoothly — 'sorry, to jump in' — that is hard work bro."
(quote-as-evidence: never name the behavior, quote his own catchphrase back as the "hard work" you witnessed. Ends by handing his loaded word back rusted. DELIVERY: the quote is an act-out — say it in HIS smooth call-voice. Built in 3 compression passes: first swing was empty sarcasm carried by caps-lock tone — rule below)

**Smug friend, loud, at a party: "Bro be honest — ChatGPT writes your jokes for you, right?"**
→ "Ahhhh... now that I think of it — thanks for the idea. Do you think if GPT writes it for me I will get more than 1 view and 1 like?"
(no defense — the accusation gets filed as a helpful tip, and the dead numbers are the alibi: material this bad could only be handmade. The question is a fork trap — "yes" means the jokes are beyond AI repair, "no" dissolves his own accusation. Sincerely consulting your accuser = deepest mask. "Ahhhh, thanks for the idea" is an act-out beat — fake realization, keep it. Callback to the 1-view-1-like keeper: the bank is starting to feed itself)

**Friend, soft consoling voice, in front of the group: "Don't worry bro, views aren't everything. I'm sure at least your family enjoys the podcast."**
→ "I have 4 members in my family and still I have 1 view."
(pity-sink: accept the consolation and drown in it with true arithmetic — the room does the subtraction and realizes even the household voted no. First draft ended "does that answer your Q?" — a courtroom flex that snapped the pitiful mask; cut it and "1 view" lands last. The pitiful man doesn't win the exchange, he loses it so hard the consoler looks cruel)

**Party acquaintance, 20 minutes of acquaintance: "You know what your problem is? You overthink everything. Comedy should just come naturally, bro."**
→ "You are right — overthinking comes naturally to me."
(fold-back: his diagnosis and his prescription welded into one sentence — "naturally" comes back carrying the disease it was meant to cure. 2-second-clock speed, full agreement, true to the bone. The nerd identity owned as fact instead of defended)

**Rules from session 5 (mask discipline):**
- An empty suit is as dead as a slipped mask: agreement with no true payload inside is sarcasm carried by tone alone. If the words say nothing, the smirk is doing the insulting — and caps-lock/emphasis IS a smirk in text form.
- Quote-as-evidence: when you don't want to name the behavior, quote the person's own recurring catchphrase back at them. The room has heard it 400 times; the receipts do the pointing.
- A quoted catchphrase is an act-out — deliver it in THEIR voice, not yours. The impression is half the stab.

**Rules from session 6:**
- Callback chips wear out: the 1-view receipt got self-retired after two swings in one session — a repeated receipt becomes a tell. Rotate the bank.
- Warm-target law, reconfirmed twice: anything pointing below the belt ("funny in the bedroom") beats HER, not the pitch. Shame test catches it — if she'd go red, the frame is wrong.
- Receipt order law: credential first, punch list last. A receipt trailing after the laugh is an explanation, and explanations die.
- Ladder frame for "funny at home" pitches: don't jump to stage/YouTube — scout the tiny domestic rungs (society gathering, sangeet, mundan). The funny lives in the granularity.
- Drill pitches must come with receipts attached (his catch: you can't run the truth filter on an invented friend).

## The 2-second skill

1. Find the loaded word — one word carries the poison.
2. Break it: agree with it / take it literally / flip it back.

**Rule from the session: hits are TRUE, misses are invented.** When cornered, reach for the true thing, not the wild thing. Truth arrives faster and cuts deeper.

**Rules from session 2:**
- Comebacks are two lines max. Third beat = set material, not hallway material.
- Brags bounce off — the room sides with the heckler. Twist it so it costs YOU.
- Watch the third-flip dodge: the flip is the strong right hand, but over-relying on it means avoiding the self-turn. Throw the left.
- Vague self-deprecation reads as fishing for pity; specific self-deprecation reads as a joke.
