# How to comeback — the playbook
*(compiled Jul 11, 2026; updated Jul 12 with sessions 4–5. Keepers live in `My_comebacks.md`, pro examples in `best_comebacks.md`.)*

---

## The 2-second skill (the core of everything)

1. **Find the loaded word.** One word carries the poison ("real" job, "little" podcast, "stuck" in traffic).
2. **Break it:** agree with it / take it literally / flip it back.

When cornered, reach for the TRUE thing, not the wild thing. Truth arrives faster and cuts deeper.

---

## The laws (apply to every line)

- **Hits are TRUE, misses are invented.** Every line that died in sparring was invented (wetting pants, gf cheating, cardiac arrest). Every keeper was true-shaped.
- **Two lines max.** Third beat = set material, not hallway material. Move it to the Set pile.
- **Punch word last.** Nothing after the kill — no "perhaps that is why you are late."
- **Never explain the joke.** Explanation after the punch is a white flag.
- **Brags bounce.** The room sides with the heckler. Twist it so it costs YOU ("I had sex today — haven't heard that compliment in a year").
- **Specific > vague.** Vague self-deprecation reads as fishing for pity; specific reads as a joke. "1 view, 1 like — both were mine."
- **Composure reads as status.** Sometimes one word is the whole comeback ("Bigger.").

---

## DEFENSE — when the insult comes at you

1. **Attack the assumption.** Don't defend — the premise itself is the weakness.
   > "You've changed." → "Yes, one of us has to."

2. **Agree + escalate.** Own the insult harder than they threw it, with numbers.
   > "You're not funny." → "I know — 30 jokes on YouTube. 1 view, 1 like. Both were mine."
   > (Spade's move: "No, I'm not offended — 37 movies, 5 sitcoms, 2 Grammys.")

3. **Outsource the insult to a crueler heckler.**
   > "You're not funny." → "I know — my wife tells me every morning."

4. **Deliberately misunderstand / take it literally.**
   > "Nice shirt." → "Thanks, it has sleeves."

5. **Flip their word back.** They must account for the thing they don't have.
   > "How's the little podcast?" → "How's your bigger one?"
   > (Churchill's move: "I'd put poison in your coffee" → "If I were your husband, I'd drink it.")

6. **Change the frame / exaggerate.**
   > "You're late." → "Yeah, I got stuck behind my own poor life choices."

7. **Unexpected comparison.** What the joke brain already does.
   > "You're quiet today." → "I'm buffering."

8. **Own their word as your legal name.**
   > "Anyone can do a podcast." → "I know — I'm that anyone. One guy watched me for exactly 13 seconds."

**Watch the third-flip dodge:** the flip is the strong right hand, but over-relying on it means avoiding the self-turn. Throw the left.

## OFFENSE — the 5 roast shells

A roast is an **observation with teeth**: the exaggeration is fake, the direction is TRUE. Aim at what everyone already noticed — you're just the first to say it.

1. **Evidence-testifies.** Their body/stuff snitches on them. Don't state the evidence — say what it *proves*. A detective doesn't say "there's blood here"; he says what the blood means.
   > "Yeah, traffic. Your breath says you woke up half an hour ago."

2. **Backhanded compliment.** Sincere praise aimed one inch off-target — the *reason* for the praise is the wound. First half warm, second half detonates.
   > "Congratulations brother, such a good RoI. In 24 months exactly 2 phones would be better than that depreciating asset."

3. **Sarcastic over-agreement.** Accept their premise as 100% true and extend the logic one step past sanity. **LAW: never switch sides.** The insult arrives through the logic; your face stays on their team. Mask slips = death (the "5 meals," "the plate," "cardiac arrest" corpses).
   > "Yes — that Diet Coke has a negative calorific value."

4. **Comparison roast.** Name the trait → ask who else has that exact trait *professionally* → zoom in three clicks of specificity. **Trap:** comparing them to someone cool is a compliment — the roast lives in the gear-vs-mission MISMATCH.
   > "Marines wear those exact glasses to wars. You wear them to walls."

5. **Fake sympathy.** Their *choice* becomes a tragedy done TO them. You never judge the thing — your worry implies everything. **The insult stays silent** — saying "insane" or "you need therapy" is just an insult with extra steps.
   > "Brother, are you okay? Whoever did that to you clearly does not like you."

**Malice beats incompetence.** "He doesn't like you" wounds the friend; "he can't stick a patch" wounds the barber. Aim the wound at the target.

## Signature moves (the fingerprints — these are YOURS)

- **"Exactly" + audit-precise number.** Cartoon-big numbers say "I'm joking"; precise numbers say "I believe this." Precision is the funnier lie. (exactly 13 seconds, exactly 2 phones)
- **Timeline-math collision.** Two clocks that contradict: 45 min late vs woke up 30 min ago; 24-month EMI vs 12-month release cycle.
- **Loaded-word flip.** Same word, new location: "stuck in traffic" → "stuck between a mattress and a blanket."
- **Spec-sheet consultant voice.** Deliver absurdity as a lab report: negative calorific value, RoI, depreciating asset. The day job is a weapon.

## Session 4 rules (Jul 12 — speed rounds)

- **Use the PROP in the scene.** The deadliest reattribution weapon is already in their hand. Drunk friend blames his knee → "beer can do that to you." Look at what they're holding before you look anywhere else.
- **Anti-joke burden-swap.** When someone demands a free sample ("say something funny"), volunteer the OTHER dreaded free sample back ("I'm also a software consultant — any software issues I can fix for you?"). Refusing the audition IS the joke.
- **Faux-fascination needs receipts.** Fake ignorance ("triathlon? Haven't heard about that all week") lands as sarcasm ONLY because the room counted the 9 mentions. No shared receipts = you just sound uninformed.
- **Beloved elders: the win condition is HER laugh.** You never beat an aunty — you answer her question by missing the point perfectly, with warmth. Deranged receipts as proof of seriousness ("3 GitHub repos full of jokes") keep it loving.

## Session 5 rules (Jul 12 — mask discipline)

- **An empty suit is as dead as a slipped mask.** Agreement with no true payload inside is sarcasm carried by tone alone — if the words say nothing, the smirk is doing the insulting. And caps-lock/emphasis IS a smirk in text form. Every mask needs a knife: one true, specific thing inside the agreement.
- **Quote-as-evidence.** When you don't want to name the behavior, quote the person's own recurring catchphrase back at them ("'sorry, to jump in' — that is hard work bro"). The room has heard it 400 times; the receipts do the pointing, the word "interrupt" never appears.
- **A quoted catchphrase is an act-out.** Deliver it in THEIR voice, not yours. The impression is half the stab.
- **The fork-trap question.** Turn the punch into a sincere question aimed back at the accuser ("do you think if GPT writes it I'll get more than 1 view?"). Either answer digs them deeper — and sincerely consulting your accuser is the deepest version of the mask.
- **Pity-sink: lose harder.** When consoled, don't stand up — drown, with true arithmetic ("4 family members, still 1 view"). The pitiful man doesn't win the exchange; he loses it so hard the consoler looks cruel.
- **Fold-back their own vocabulary.** Weld their diagnosis and their prescription into one sentence ("overthinking comes naturally to me"). No new words needed — their sentence carried both halves of the joke.
- **Tails snap masks.** The slips have moved: no longer mid-line, now they arrive as tails — the explaining beat ("he is not coming to me"), the courtroom flex ("does that answer your Q?"). The line ends where the kill lands. Cut everything after.

## When stuck

- **Re-name the trait by FUNCTION.** If every answer comes from one category, the trait was named too narrowly. "Wants to look like a celeb" → only celebs. "Wearing eye protection against nothing" → the whole world opens.
- **Blank-fill drill.** List 5 boring answers fast, don't try to be funny, then shop the list. The kill hides among the duds ("wall" looked like the dud → wars/walls).
- **Park it.** Capture beats grinding — "real jobs pay you" arrived 10 minutes after parking. It arrives in the auto to Powai.
- **Mouth, not pen.** Say it out loud 3 times fast. Soft connective tissue falls off on its own.
