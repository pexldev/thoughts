## Types of comedy

1. Recognition
Audience thinks: “Haan yaar, this is true.”
Example: YouTube feels productive even when it wastes time.

2. Misdirection
You take them one way, then suddenly turn.
“Podcasts are self-improvement…” → “No, lullabies for broken adults.”

3. Specificity
Generic: “I watch podcasts to sleep.”
Funny: “A 47-year-old guy named Dave explaining supply chain logistics is the only thing between me and 2014.”

4. Exaggeration
You stretch truth till it becomes absurd but still believable.
“Brain sees notification like bush rustling.”

5. Vulnerability
You admit something embarrassing before audience can judge you.
“I quit Spanish because I imagined dying in Colombia after saying gracias.”

6. Compression
Funny dies when overexplained. The shorter, sharper version hits harder.
“Instagram gives guilt. YouTube gives false hope.” That’s a joke.

7. Rhythm
Setup → pause → punch.
Sometimes even a mediocre line becomes funny because of delivery.


## Punchline
🔥 4 punchline formulas you can use
1. Expectation Flip (most common)


Structure:
👉 “You think X… but actually Y”

Example:
“I thought I was improving my life…
I was just avoiding it in HD.”

2. Specific Absurd Detail

Structure:
👉 normal → hyper-specific weird image

Example:
“A 47-year-old guy named Dave explaining supply chain logistics…”

3. Self-Destruction

Structure:
👉 build yourself up → destroy yourself

Example:
“I started learning Spanish…
I quit because I imagined dying in Colombia after saying gracias.”

4. Analogy / Metaphor

Structure:
👉 compare to something unexpected

Example:
“Notifications are just digital bushes rustling.”

🔪 Final step: tighten

Take your punchline and ask:
👉 “Can I remove 3–5 words?”

Shorter = sharper = funnier.

🧠 Your personal cheat code

Every time you write, do this:

👉 Write 1 setup
👉 Write 3 different punchlines for it

1. Always ask: “Expected kya hai?” → then break it
    Expected: “Big car = success”
    Twist: “Big car = 0–0 in Mumbai traffic”
2. Force stupid comparisons daily
    Not smart ones. Stupid ones.
    “Rickshaws are like weather” — that’s gold because it’s illogical but true.
    Your brain learns unpredictability by practicing wrong connections.
3. Delay the obvious punchline
    Your brain jumps to “logical joke” → reject it.
    Push one step further:
    Not “people buy SUVs for status”
    But “people buy SUVs so security guards respect them more than interns”
