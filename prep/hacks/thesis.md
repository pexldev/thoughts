# Thesis Generation

Thesis generation can also be reduced to a few repeatable questions.

---

## Hack 1 — What is this actually about?

Keep replacing the answer with something more abstract.

**Example:** Fat guy with chips.

↓ Food  
↓ Consumption  
↓ Self-control  
↓ Abundance  
↓ **Human nature**

The last 2–3 levels are usually where the thesis lives.

---

## Hack 2 — What belief does this reveal?

**Example:** Guy carrying chips everywhere.

Possible beliefs:

- *"I'll need this later."*
- *"Better to have extra."*
- *"Convenience beats discipline."*

Any of these can become a thesis.

---

## Hack 3 — What human tendency is hiding here?

Fill in the blank:

> **Humans tend to ________.**

Some examples to prime it:

- Filling empty space
- Seeking comfort
- Avoiding uncertainty
- Showing status
- Optimizing convenience

That's usually a thesis.

---

## Hack 4 — "This isn't about X. It's about ________." ⭐

Finish the sentence:

> **This isn't about chips. It's about ________.**

- comfort
- temptation
- preparation
- abundance
- lack of self-control

This forces abstraction. Fast and reliable.

---

## Hack 5 — If this happened everywhere, what would the rule be?

**Example:** Cargo pockets get filled.

**Rule:** Humans fill whatever capacity they have.

Now that's a thesis.

---

## Hack 6 — What advice would this observation accidentally support?

**Example:** Cargo pockets full of chips.

**Advice:** Don't make bad habits convenient.

That's almost a thesis.

---

## The Three to Memorize

1. **What is this actually about?**
2. **This isn't about X. It's about ________.** ⭐ *(favorite)*
3. **What human tendency does this reveal?**

Those three will get you surprisingly far.

The thesis is almost always one level more abstract than the observation.
