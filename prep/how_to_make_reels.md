# How to make a reel / teaser out of an episode
<!-- Built Jul 13 2026 after the ep18 reel session got jargon-heavy and confusing. This is the plain-language version. The scorecard in ops/reels_that_work.md is the ADVANCED layer — ignore it until this feels easy. -->

## THE ONE PICTURE

**An episode is a meal. A reel is a free sample handed out at the door.** The sample's only job: make them come in for the meal.

Two kinds of sample:
- **A CUT (a reel):** a real bite sliced from the finished meal. Made AFTER recording (cut from real audio). Runs AFTER the episode is out.
- **A TEASER:** not food — a photo of the dish with steam rising. A tiny trailer. Made BEFORE, to announce the meal. Runs BEFORE the episode.

Tonight's confusion, solved: the "numbers" rewrite served the WHOLE dish, so it's a reel, not a teaser. A teaser that gives away the punchline has served the meal — it's not a teaser anymore.

## HOW TO FIND A REEL INSIDE AN EPISODE (the real skill)

Play any 30-sec chunk. Ask 3 questions:
1. **Stand alone?** Makes sense to someone who never heard the rest?
2. **Hook in 2 seconds?** Opens on something wild/tense — NOT "so this happened the other day"?
3. **Sendable?** Can I finish "send this to your ___" with a real person-type?

**All three yes = it's a reel.** Most of the episode FAILS this — normal. Hunt for the 2–3 that survive being ripped out of context.
(Ep18 winners: the wife's taunt run / the 3 AM audit / the VS Code bit — each passed all three.)

## HOW TO SHAPE IT

1. **Start on the most interesting line** — rip it to the front even if I said it mid-bit.
2. **Delete anything that needs context** from elsewhere.
3. **End HARD on the funniest line.** No "so yeah," no outro breath.
4. **Put the words on screen** — most people watch muted.

## TEASER — one extra rule

It's a trailer: **show the steam, stop before the best bite.** Tease the situation, cut BEFORE the punchline. (If a teaser makes people feel done, it gave away too much — that's a reel wearing a teaser's coat.)

## THE PROCESS (every episode)

1. Record the episode.
2. Read it back; mark every spot that passes the 3-question test.
3. Pick the top 2–3.
4. Each one: start on the hook → cut the context → end on the punch → stamp the text.
5. One of those, OR a fresh 20-sec trailer, goes out BEFORE the episode as the teaser.
