# How to talk to the mic — flow drills for 10–15 min on any topic
<!-- Built Jul 13 2026. The skill under the whole ladder: host → engaging guest → stage. All three are just "talk out loud, engagingly, no script." -->

## The three ways solo mic talk dies

**You dry up. You go abstract. You go stiff.** Every hack kills one of these. Diagnose which one is killing a take, apply the matching fix.

## THE CORE REFRAME

**I'm not talking ON a topic. I'm telling ONE person about ONE moment.**

- **"One moment" kills drying-up.** A topic is infinite and empty (30 seconds, then nothing). A specific scene has details I can walk around inside for 10 minutes. The topic is just a DOOR — walk through it into a real memory and never look back at the door. (This is also Wednesday Law 1 and the "lived scene" from how_to_structure_wednesday.md.)
- **"One person" kills stiffness** (my flat/list-y delivery tell). A mic is a void and I *perform* to a void. Put a photo of ONE real friend next to the mic and just tell them. Voice unclenches instantly.

## THE HACKS

1. **The question engine (my secret weapon).** When I dry up, the fuel is a QUESTION. I already own the whole /spar arsenal — point it at myself, out loud, on mic: "Why does this bother me? When did I first notice it? Who's the victim — now flip it? What would the other person say back? How am I the exact thing I'm complaining about?" Each question buys ~90 more seconds. Never run dry if I keep asking.

2. **Three gears (built-in 10-min arc):** Surface (general observation) → My life (how it shows up for me) → Confession (the embarrassing thing I've never said). Drying-up usually = stuck on gear one. Descend.

3. **NEVER STOP THE TAPE.** (Biggest one for me — the overthinker who wants to retake.) In practice, restarting is BANNED. Fumble? Say "let me say that better" on mic and keep rolling. The retake instinct and the flow muscle are enemies. Not making an episode — training an engine.

4. **Runway phrases** — connective lines that buy time AND push forward: "and here's the thing…" / "but it gets worse…" / "and I'll be honest with you…" / "let me back up." Gear-changes, not filler.

5. **Land it, don't just stop.** Close on a feeling or a question. But never let needing-an-ending stop me from starting. Start ugly, find the ending on the way.

## THE REPEATABLE DRILL

1. Random topic (pull from ideas.md or a word generator).
2. 60 sec prep — prep ONE thing only: **the moment/scene.** Not structure, not jokes. (Pass 1 = generate; don't structure while talking.)
3. Record. 10 min. No stopping, no restarting.
4. Play back once. Log only: where did I dry up / go abstract / go stiff? Don't fix the take — log the pattern.
5. New topic next time. Volume beats polish (Joke Lab law).

## THE HACK THAT BEATS THE RECORDING CONSTRAINT

Separate the SKILL from the MIC. The skill = "talk continuously and engagingly." The mic is optional. I can only record when she's out — but I can run the flow muscle **off-mic anytime**: narrate the day out loud in the auto, argue with a podcast in the shower, tell the empty room about today's annoyance. The mic should never be the first time my mouth meets the topic.
