# Podcast answers — banked from Q&A sparring

The frame: **Headline → Scene → Turn → Stop.**
Headline = answer in one sentence, with attitude. Scene = ONE specific as proof (one example is a movie, three is a list). Turn = the self-cost beat. Stop = land it and hand the mic back — no "so yeah."

An engaging answer is a joke's anatomy worn casually: setup → specific → punch → punch word last. Cut the runway before the answer and the apology after it.

---

## "What's your podcast about?" (the question you'll get on every show, forever)

→ "There are 2 podcasts actually. The Wednesday podcast is where I say whatever I want on a Wednesday and then regret it on a Thursday — so I just named it I Could Be Wrong. Then there's Joke Lab: 2 observations and 20 jokes. 19 bad and 1 good, maybe. You'll hear anything from 'In India we don't say hi to a stranger, but we happily marry one' to 'why am I even doing this.' Come listen to me from my bedroom — Wednesday and Friday. It's one of the very few podcasts where only one person talks, because I cannot convince anyone to come to my bedroom."

Anatomy:
- **Headline (Wednesday):** say it Wednesday, regret it Thursday — timeline collision, the title confessed as a disclaimer.
- **Headline (Joke Lab):** 19 bad and 1 good, *maybe* — brutal ratio + the "maybe" hedges even the one good joke. The whole show thesis in one hedge.
- **Scene:** the marriage-to-strangers joke + the break-character aside. The specific does the selling.
- **Plant → payoff:** "from my bedroom" planted in the CTA, paid off by the guest line. The repetition works because the punch adds the REASON.
- **Turn/Stop:** the cost lands on him (nobody will come), punch word last, no trailing mumble.

Pocket beat (optional, when the room is warm): after "regret it on a Thursday" — act-out shouting at an offended stranger: **"I TOLD YOU I'M WRONG."**

Died in drafting: "it's my channel name / I have not one but 2 podcasts" (org-chart runway), "it's like a vlog" (category word, fails the generic test), "what I learned from it" (LinkedIn bio), "oops that sounded wrong" (giggling at your own line — explanation tax).

## "What's the day job — does the office know?"

→ "I'm a software consultant, which is pretty much like my podcast — A JOKE. And I'm glad no one knows about it, because 1 joke can end my 10 years of consulting."

Anatomy:
- **Callback:** "a joke" ties back to the 19-bad-1-good answer — callbacks across an interview read as quick.
- **The specific fuels the joke:** "software consultant" (never the firm name — reflex held under fire). The room already half-believes consulting is fake; the real noun is the ammunition.
- **Signature collision:** 1 joke vs 10 years — numbers on both sides of the scale.
- Full-dodge warning: answering the day-job question with pure evasion gets it re-asked unscripted. Real answer wrapped in the joke.

## "Who are your influences?"

→ "I have a GitHub repo of just Hedberg jokes. So obviously Mitch Hedberg. Then I study the masters — Carlin, Burr. But no matter how much I study, my wife never seems to like my jokes."

Anatomy:
- **Reveal structure:** evidence first ("GitHub repo"), deadpan conclusion after ("so obviously"). The repo image answers the question AND characterizes him — the engineer who version-controls comedians.
- **Outsourced verdict:** the wife as the audience of one who remains unmoved — masters-level study vs zero domestic laughs. The gap is the punch. ENDS THERE.
- Died in drafting: "everyone is better than me" (vague self-pity), the IYKYK ellipsis, "I wonder what the solution is" (riddle in a trench coat).

## "Are you actually funny in real life?" (the boss-fight question — the honest answer is no)

→ "Ohhh no no — I'm not funny in real life. My life has fun with me. I uploaded 30 jokes on YouTube. 1 view, 1 like. Both were mine."

Anatomy:
- **The flip:** subject-object reversal — he's the joke, life is the comedian. The spiral's confession, SHAPED (fold-back family). This is the template for every "are you any good?" question: never bleed, flip.
- **The receipt:** the already-perfected banked line, verbatim — numbers escalate 30 → 1 → 1, "both were mine" lands last. First person, because it's TRUE — "imagine uploading..." was the final draft's flinch; hypothetical framing softens a confession that's stronger as fact.
- **Stop.** No "if you look at it it's funny but if I look at it..." — the audience does the sad math silently.

**Laws from session 2:**
- **Turn vs bleed:** a turn costs you and is SHAPED ("19 bad and 1 good, maybe") — the room laughs. A bleed just leaks ("IDK, maybe another life") — the room gets uncomfortable. Same confession, opposite outcomes. The spiral is not allowed stage time unshaped.
- **Commit or cut:** a spoken answer cannot contain ellipses. Either say the punch out loud or end at the last laugh. The audience never finishes the joke for you.
- Names are a census; the list is setup, the confession is the answer.

## "Describe your comedy in 3 words."

→ "I AM WRONG."

(Speed-round kill, 2 seconds. The title routed and committed — hedge dropped, full conviction about being wrong. The trap was adjectives; the answer was identity.)

## "How much of your material does AI write?" (guaranteed question, loaded — CRAFTED Jul 12)

→ "Everything actually — everything is written by AI. AI sat on that flight next to a large guy, noticed he had chips in his cargo pants, and was not able to fit on that aisle seat. So it comes to me and it says 'write a joke on this idiot,' and I go 'IDK how to write a joke, can you write this joke for me?' and then AI is like 'ok dummy, here you go — if he squeezed some air off those chips, I would have had more leg room.'"
*(tag, AFTER the laugh, light and thrown away:)* "Did you write this question, or did AI?"

Anatomy:
- **Headline:** total over-agreement — "everything, actually." The comebacks mask walked into the interview room. No defense, no denial.
- **Scene (the absurd alibi):** AI physically wedged into seat 14B, noticing cargo-pants chips. The image proves the opposite of the claim — AI didn't live his life. The lived specifics are the receipts.
- **Turn (self-cost):** in his own story AI does the noticing, the assigning, AND the writing — he's the delivery boy of his own comedy career, and even the AI calls him "dummy." The daily gym truth, shaped.
- **The act-out ends on a real sample joke** — he proves the goods mid-confession. Punch word last ("leg room").
- **Tag:** the premise escalates outward onto the host's own question, and hands the mic back as a question — the Stop built in. Delivery law: it rides after the laugh, never rushed past the punchline.

Speed-round / hallway version (still holds):
→ "Have you seen my numbers? If AI wrote my jokes, would I be getting 40 views?"

Died in drafting: "So AI wrote all the jokes" (explaining tail restating the headline), the tangled first-draft scene where the AI-in-the-seat image never formed.

## "What does your wife actually think of the podcast?" (she's a recurring character — this WILL get asked)

→ "Of course she likes it — if she liked it I would have gotten 2 views, right? No — every time I record from my bedroom, she goes out, puts on headphones... and listens to this show."
*(then, GENUINELY — not a jab:)* "Does your husband like this show?"

Anatomy:
- **Headline:** runs on the bank's own arithmetic — the room that heard "1 view" earlier does the math instantly. The bank is starting to feed itself across answers.
- **Scene:** she doesn't just skip his show — she soundproofs herself against a live recording in her own house, to consume the HOST's content instead. Betrayal + flattery in one image.
- **"This show" is a SLOT** — re-aims itself at whichever podcast he's sitting on. Custom flattery, zero new writing.
- **The question back is SINCERE, not a jab** — his correction, and it's the stronger read: the joke already landed, then he shows real curiosity about the host's life, turning the Q&A into a conversation. Engaging-guest behavior at its purest — hosts remember guests who ask back. (First draft's version WAS a jab-deflection and read as the AI answer's trick repeated; rebuilding the scene + the sincere intent is what transformed it.)
- **Mouth-cut pending:** "insists on staying out" + "goes out" = same beat twice; say it fast and one falls off.

## "What's the endgame — where do you want this to go?" (the closer question, LinkedIn-trap minefield)

→ "Well... somewhere better than 1 view. I wish it reaches a point where I can convince someone to come to my bedroom and have a conversation over 2 mics. Or someday — I do what I do on a stage, with 1 mic. If you ask me, 1 mic > 2 mics. I'd consider myself lucky if I get to do both."

Anatomy:
- **The countdown spine:** better than **1 view** → dream of **2 mics** → summit is **1 mic**. The numbers shrink as the dream gets bigger — the engineer brain stating ambition as arithmetic, zero vision-statement.
- **"1 mic > 2 mics"** — an inequality about his soul. Says "stand-up is everything" in his native notation. The conviction beat.
- **Callback payoff:** "convince someone to come to my bedroom" pays off the guest-line from the what's-it-about answer — the running gag graduates into the actual dream.
- **Ends sincere BY DESIGN:** the dream question is usually the interview's last; a punchline there reads as armor. "Lucky" keeps the ranking while admitting both are dreams — warm, no flex, mic handed back.
- Died in drafting: "do you think that is ever possible?" (a bleed — begging the host for reassurance; it existed because the answer was hiding the stage), "but if I could do both nothing like it" (wants everything, commits to nothing, un-picks the choice).

## Rules from session 1 (Jul 11)

- The first draft of "what's it about" is always the witness answer: accurate, complete, forgettable. Generic test: if 10,000 podcasters could say your sentence verbatim, it describes a category, not your show.
- The answer often falls out while explaining why you DON'T have one — mouth, not pen.
- Routing: entertaining guests aren't improvising — they route every question to pocket stories they already own. The bank is the prep.
- Schedule/CTA never gets announcer voice — it rides inside a sentence that's already doing something.

## Rules from session 3 (Jul 12)

- **The runner:** the 1-view stat now threads three answers (both were mine → I'd have 2 views → better than 1 view). Callbacks across an interview compound — the bank feeds itself. Watch the account balance: a runner must escalate or vary; pure restatement is its weakest use.
- **Question-back endings:** asking the host a question can be the Stop — but only when it's sincere curiosity or rides the answer's own premise outward. Three in a row = the host sees the formula. Vary the exits.
- **A reassurance-question is a leak detector:** "do you think it's possible?" existed because the answer was hiding the stage. When the ending begs, the middle is lying — say the truer beat and the question mark becomes a period.
- **One sincere ending per interview:** the dream/endgame question (usually last) is the one answer allowed to end warm instead of funny — a punchline there reads as armor.
- **Mask transfer confirmed:** the comebacks over-agreement mask works in the interview room (the AI answer) — same discipline, longer leash.
