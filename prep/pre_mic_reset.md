# Pre-mic reset — get out of threat mode BEFORE you talk
<!-- Built Jul 15 2026. Companion to how_to_talk_to_mic.md. Source spark: daniel_berada/how-to-talk-2-urself.md (kept the nervous-system protocol, dropped the manifestation woo). -->

## The one idea

**Nerves are a body-state, not a thoughts-problem. You can't out-talk them — you down-regulate the body first, THEN speak.**

When I'm nervous on mic I'm in fight-or-flight (sympathetic). In that state my body is sending a "danger" signal that drowns out any words I say to myself — which is why "just be confident" bounces, and why my delivery goes stiff/flat. The fix isn't a better pep talk. It's flipping the body to calm (parasympathetic) so the loose, funny brain comes back online. **Body first, words last.**

## THE 90-SECOND RESET (do this before every record)

1. **Breathe it down — 3 to 5 rounds.** Inhale deep through the nose; at the top, sneak in one extra quick sip of air. Then exhale *slow and long* — longer than the inhale. Make an **"F" sound** to stretch the exhale out. The long exhale is what physically trips the switch from threat → calm (vagus nerve). By round 3–4 something in the chest/shoulders lets go — that's the switch flipping.
2. **Relabel, out loud: "I'm amped, not nervous."** Nervous and excited are the *same body* — pounding heart, buzz. Don't fight for "calm" (too big a lie). Just rename the exact feeling I already have. Same fuel, better story.
3. **Self-compassion, one line: "Allowed to be rough today."** Takes the gun off my own head so the engine can idle. Not hype — permission.
4. **Aim, don't script:** photo of ONE friend by the mic; pick the ONE moment/scene (not the topic). (Per how_to_talk_to_mic.md.)
5. **Hit record — and NEVER STOP THE TAPE.** Say it once, don't dig up the seed to check if it grew. The retake instinct IS the nerves winning. Fumble → "let me say that better" on mic, keep rolling.

## Why forced positive talk fails (and what to do instead)

- **Cut the negative before adding the positive.** One "fuck me, I'm bad at this" does more damage than ten "I'm great"s can repair. Highest-leverage move = kill the global self-attack, not manufacture affirmations. (See the global-self-attack entry in /motivate: drag "I'm bad" → "what, exactly, missed this time?")
- **If I do speak well to myself, keep it TRUE and SPECIFIC, from a calm body.** "I built two A-jokes at 2 AM the night I said I couldn't" lands because it happened. "I'm an amazing comedian" bounces because my body knows it's a stretch. Evidence doesn't trigger the internal argument; inflation does.

## Beats-the-constraint note

The breath reset + relabel needs no mic and no privacy. Run it before off-mic flow drills in the auto/shower too (I can only record when she's out, but the muscle trains anytime — same law as how_to_talk_to_mic.md). Every rep raises the baseline so eventually I drop into calm in one or two breaths.
