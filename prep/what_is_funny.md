# What is funny — the craft card
<!-- Built Aug 15 2026. The card under all the other cards. Companion to how_to_write_an_essay.md, how_to_make_reels.md, how_to_talk_to_mic.md. -->

Every rule in my lab notes is downstream of this one page. If I ever forget why a rule exists, the answer is here.

---

## 1. Comedy is a prediction machine

While I talk, the audience is quietly predicting where the sentence is going. They can't help it — brains predict, constantly, without permission.

> **A joke = build the prediction cheaply, break it in one word, and the broken version turns out to be true.**

That's the whole thing. Everything else is machinery for building the prediction cheaply and breaking it late.

## 2. The formula: SURPRISE + RECOGNITION

| What I gave them | What I get |
|---|---|
| Surprise only (didn't see it coming, isn't true) | Confusion. They stare. |
| Recognition only (true, but they saw it coming) | A nod. "So true." That's a tweet. **Nobody laughs at a tweet.** |
| **Both at once** | **The laugh.** |

The laugh feels like being *caught*. I didn't teach them anything new — I said the thing they already had and never put words on.

**Diagnostic:** when a line dies, it's almost always missing one of the two. Ask which. If it's missing recognition, the joke isn't about their life. If it's missing surprise, they got there before I did — the turn arrived too early or the setup gave it away.

## 3. Funny is not in the topic. It's in the GAP.

The gap between **what gets said** and **what's true**.

So my job is not inventing funny things. It's **finding gaps that already exist and pointing at them.**

> *"They call that Death by Chocolate. Which is accurate."*

I didn't write that punchline. It sat on a dessert menu for thirty years. Everyone read it a hundred times and nobody heard it. That's the Carlin move — **the language was already the joke.**

This is also why the show's format works: a saying every Indian repeats IS a gap, pre-installed, with the whole audience already holding it.

## 4. The three parts, and the only job each one has

| Part | Its only job |
|---|---|
| **Setup** | Load a prediction. Cheap, fast, **no jokes inside it**. One sentence. Two sentences = it's a story, not a joke. |
| **Turn** | Break the prediction in one word. That word goes **LAST**. |
| **Tag** | Stay in the new world one beat longer — smaller, stranger. **Re-frames, never explains.** |

**Why punch word last isn't a style preference:** the laugh is involuntary and it starts the instant the prediction breaks. Anything I say after it gets driven over by the noise. I'm not being elegant — I'm getting out of the way.

**Why no punchline word may appear in the setup:** the word is the trigger. Fire it early and there's nothing left to break.

## 5. Comedy needs a victim

Somebody has to be exposed. That exposure is the pain that makes it comedy and not just an observation. But pain only reads as funny when it's **safe** — old, small, or mine.

**My standing answer is: me.** Not humility — licence. It's what lets me be preachy and stay likeable, and it's the whole reason the self-turn is mandatory in every file.

It's also why Flat 901 is parked. The victim there is a real person in current pain. No safety, no comedy — just cruelty with timing.

## 6. The test — one line

> ### What were they predicting, and at exactly which word did it break?

If I can't answer that about a line I wrote, **it isn't a joke yet — it's a true statement.** True statements are where jokes come from, not what jokes are.

## 7. The four killers (all the same crime)

All four are me filling the gap myself instead of letting them fall into it:

1. **Explaining.** The tag that says why it's funny.
2. **Stating the emotion.** *"It broke me." "I felt something."* — my oldest tell, and it always shows up exactly at the embarrassing part. Give behaviour and objects; they'll supply the feeling, and the feeling they supply is the laugh.
3. **Announcing.** *"And there's this one line that stuck with me…"* — now they're braced. Braced audiences don't get surprised.
4. **Words after the punch.** Covered above.

> *"Congratulations guys. I'm so happy for you."* works because the gap stays open.
> *"It broke me"* got cut because it hands them the answer.

## 8. The jargon, last (so I can read other people's craft books)

The three classic theories of humour — worth knowing, only one is useful:

- **Superiority** (Hobbes): we laugh at someone below us. Explains bullying comedy. Ages badly.
- **Relief** (Freud): laughter discharges built-up tension. Explains why the room laughs *harder* after a long pause, and why a laugh breaks after something taboo gets said out loud.
- **Incongruity** (Kant, Schopenhauer): we laugh when a strongly-held expectation resolves into something that doesn't fit — but fits *better*. **This is the one. This is the prediction machine.**

The working name for the trick in most books is **misdirection**, and for the mechanism, **the incongruity-resolution model.** I don't need the words to do it. I need them to argue with a book.

## The checks

- [ ] Can I name the prediction, and the exact word that broke it?
- [ ] Is it **true** as well as surprising? (recognition test)
- [ ] Is the punch word **last**?
- [ ] Is someone exposed — and is it **me**?
- [ ] Did I explain, state the emotion, announce, or talk past the laugh?
