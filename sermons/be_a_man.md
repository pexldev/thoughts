> **The commandment:** You have to **be a man.**
>
> **Who says it to you:** Your father. Your uncles. Your friends at the table. Every man who has ever handed you a drink you didn't ask for.
>
> **The permission you're giving:** ⚠️ **INVERTED — see below.**

**Four-second test: passes clean.** *"You have to be a man"* is a commandment, and you can name five people who've said it to you. Target stands. It's also sitting in `ideas.md` twice already — *"Who is a man? why does he always have to be mean?"* and *"Dont be humble, everyone on internet is advising against being a good boy."*

---

## ⚠️ THIS ONE INVERTS THE FORMAT — read before writing blocks

Corporate's guru gave permission **NOT** to obey. *Skip the meeting. Nobody cares.*

This guru gives permission **TO obey — all the way down.** He doesn't argue with "be a man." He agrees enthusiastically and then keeps walking off the edge. *Grow some balls → slap someone → drink more → drive → die.*

**Same machine, opposite polarity.** It still works, and here's why: the corporate sermon's joke was in the *permission*. This one's joke is in the **ladder** — obedience taken literally until it kills you. The audience does the arguing, not you.

**What the inversion costs you:** the escalation now has to be airtight, because it's carrying the whole bit. In corporate, a flat block still had a funny permission in it. Here, a rung that doesn't cost more than the one before it is dead weight — the ladder is the only structure holding this up.

**On the drunk-driving rung:** it's satire and it reads as satire *because* the ladder ends in a body. The ending is what makes it anti-, not pro-. Don't soften the end to be safe — softening it is the only thing that would make it unsafe.

### What's already structurally right in it

- **The ladder exists and the rungs escalate.** *balls → slap → fuck up → drink → drive → die.* That's the hardest part of a sermon and you did it in one pass, cold, two days after saying your muscles were weak.
- **"Drink more" repeats three times, evenly spaced.** That's the refrain, found by feel.
- **The drink/throw up/drink loop** is a real comic figure — the loop *is* the joke, because a loop can't escalate and that's the point of it.
- **"We all r going to die"** — the only line in the dump that isn't second person. It's a first-person-plural slip, and it's sitting **one line from the end.** That is exactly where THE CRACK goes. Right instinct, right place, by accident.
- **"Die a man not a pussy"** closes the ladder on the commandment itself. The bit eats its own thesis.

### What needs fixing before blocks

1. **"Sometimes it's ok to be man"** — *sometimes* and *it's ok* are both hedges. The guru never hedges; he has no doubt, that's the whole character. This is a podcast opening, not a sermon opening.
2. **No objects.** See above. This is the work.
3. **Second person drifts.** *"Be messy," "fuck up"* are fine. *"We all r going to die"* is the one legal exception — everything else must stay **you.**
4. **Two rungs are vaguer than their neighbours:** *"Be messy"* and *"fuck up"* are categories, not acts. *Slap someone* is an act. *Throw up* is an act. Those two need to become something a body does.
5. **THE FLOOR (block 7) isn't in the dump.** No command, no joke, no permission — the cold true thing about what this costs a man, then the refrain alone. Nothing in these 21 lines stops for it. That block is where the episode earns the ending.
