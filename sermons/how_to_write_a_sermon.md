# How To Write A Sermon
*The machine, derived from your own corporate script. Every example below is a line you already wrote.*

---

## The actual problem — you don't have an idea problem

`ideas.md` has ~110 topics in it. You are not short of ideas.

You're short of the same thing `/daily-reps` found in August: **not material, stems.** You can't tell, looking at that list, which line is a sermon and which isn't — and once you pick one, you're entering it from scratch every time.

And there's a second thing making it harder than it needs to be: **your prep template is built for the old format.** [joke-lab/prep/_template.md](../joke-lab/prep/_template.md) is a 7-level interrogation that turns one topic into ten separate one-liners. That's Joke Lab. SERMONS is not a list of jokes — it's **one continuous voice running linear scenes.** You've been writing a new format with the old scaffold. Of course it feels hard.

So: two machines below. One turns any idea into a sermon target. One turns a target into blocks.

# PART 1 — THE TARGET FINDER

## The sermon eats exactly one shape of input: a commandment

Not a topic. Not an observation. **A thing people tell you that you have to do.**

**The test — one sentence:**

> Can you say it as **"You have to ______"**?

- "snoring" → *You have to... snore?* No. **Not a sermon.** That's a one-liner.
- "work is worship" → *You have to treat work like worship.* **Yes. Sermon.**
- "solo trip" → *You have to take a solo trip to find yourself.* **Yes. Sermon.**
- "loneliness in mumbai" → no commandment. **Not a sermon.** That's a Wednesday.

That test is the whole filter. It takes four seconds per idea and it's the thing you've been doing by instinct and therefore slowly.

## Three ways to find the commandment when it's hiding

**1. The saying IS the commandment.** Free targets, zero conversion.
*karm karo fal ki chinta mat karo · work is worship · an eye for an eye · penny wise pound foolish · you are what you eat · grass is always greener · the more you fuck around the more you find out*

**2. Name who says it to you.** If you can name the person, it's a target.
Your manager. Your dad. LinkedIn. The aunty. The guy on a podcast. If nobody says it, there's no authority to parody — and the sermon is a parody of an authority.

**3. Find the commandment underneath the complaint.**
Your idea is usually the *symptom*. The target is the *instruction* that caused it.
> "Why do I never feel productive?" → the commandment is **"You have to be productive."**
> "A real good hobby loses money" → the commandment is **"You have to have a hobby."**
> "People prefer murder over saying no" → the commandment is **"You can't say no."**

**If it passes, it goes in [target_bank.md](target_bank.md). I've already run your whole `ideas.md` through this — 14 targets are sitting there ready.**

# PART 2 — THE BLOCK MACHINE

## You already built this. You just didn't name it.

I pulled apart all nine blocks of the corporate script. **Eight of them are the identical five-beat move.** You did it by feel. Named, you can run it forever.

### The five beats

| # | Beat | What it does | Your line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **THE OBJECT** | Name one **specific physical thing in their day.** With a detail — a time, a number, a colour. | *"That meeting that you have 1.30 PM today"* |
| **2** | **THE COMMAND** | One imperative. No hedging. No explaining. | *"Skip it. Fuck it."* |
| **3** | **THE REASON** | The authority line. Stated as fact, never argued. | *"Nobody cares."* |
| **4** | **THE ESCALATION** | Go one rung bolder, or run the scene forward in real time. | *"Take a half day. Tell them you are sick."* |
| **5** | **THE EXIT** | Something small and physical. Usually food. | *"New season of Lock Up is out. Watch that. And enjoy your lunch."* |

### It holds across every block

**Block 5 — MANAGE UPWARD**
> **Object:** "That spreadsheet that you are working on"
> **Command:** "Tag your manager. Not your lead. Your manager."
> **Reason:** "Your lead is the lawyer. Your manager is the judge."
> **Escalation:** "That 1-1 your manager set up — that's your day in court."
> **Exit:** "Then order an ice cream. A nice death by chocolate."

**Block 6 — THE CERTIFICATION**
> **Object:** "That one certification?" · **Command:** "Don't."
> **Reason:** "AI can do that certification. And AI will probably score better than you."
> **Escalation:** "He already knows who he's promoting."
> **Exit:** "Go make reels on Instagram."

### Beat 1 is the one that decides everything

Look at what you instinctively did — you never opened a block on a topic. You opened on **an object in the viewer's hands, right now:**

> that meeting at **1.30 today** · that **deck** · your project **in red** · that **call you're getting** · that **spreadsheet** · that **one certification** · the ID **that hangs around your neck** · that rice you cooked **four days ago**

Not "meetings are pointless." **"That meeting that you have 1.30 PM today."** The time is doing the work. The viewer checks their calendar.

This is the same finding as the reels memo — *witnessed, not relatable* — and it's the thing that makes the first 3 seconds work. **Write beat 1 first. Always. Before you know the joke.**

### Beat 3 is where your signature move lives

*"Your lead is the lawyer. Your manager is the judge."*
*"You're 112345."*

That's the emotional thing described in the precise vocabulary of a system, straight-faced. Beat 3 is the slot for it. When a block feels flat, it's almost always because beat 3 is an opinion instead of a system.

## The four rules that hold the voice together

1. **Second person, the whole way.** *You.* Never "I", never "we", never "us" — until the one crack in block 9. The moment you say "I think", the guru is gone and it's a podcast again.
2. **Never explain. Never advise.** The parked list is instructive — you cut *"You have nothing to lose everything to gain"* and *"don't die because someone wants you to be one way"* for exactly this. Those are sermons in the bad sense. The guru gives **permission**, never **advice**.
3. **The refrain lands about every two blocks.** *"Nobody cares."* Your own note flagged it: it goes quiet for four minutes in the middle. That's the thread the audience holds.
4. **The runner exits the blocks.** Food, three times: Lock Up → ice cream → butter chicken. You had six and cut to three. Three is right.

# PART 3 — THE EPISODE SHAPE

**8 blocks of escalating permission, then the turn.**

The ladder across corporate: *skip a meeting → fake a stat → refuse the weekend → take a competitor's call → work your manager → refuse the certification → **you are a number** → go home and eat.* Each rung costs more than the last.

**Two blocks break the pattern on purpose — and they're the two that matter:**

**THE FLOOR (block 7).** Drop the command. No permission, no joke. Just the cold true thing, then the refrain alone.
> *"You're not Tina. You're not Natasha. You're not David. You're 112345." → [BEAT] → "Nobody cares."*

**THE CRACK (block 9).** The persona slips into first person. **Once.** Two lines.
> *"I'm trying. But nothing is working."*

Then it recovers and exits soft. You cut *"But I will keep trying"* so the slip stays a fragment — that was the right call and it's the single best instinct in the script. **Don't let the crack heal.**

# PART 4 — THE UNLOCK: one block = one reel

**This is the part that makes the whole system pay.**

A sermon block is already reel-shaped: one object, one command, one escalation, one exit. It's a standalone scene. It doesn't need the episode around it.

So you are not writing an episode and then separately writing reels. **You write eight blocks. Two become the week's reels. All eight become the episode.** One writing act, two outputs. That's the answer to "how do I keep feeding the sprint."

**Picking which block becomes the reel:** the one with the strongest **object** — most specific, most people hold it. Not the one with the best punchline. The recruiter block is the obvious lead (everyone gets that call).

**Two hard specs when a block becomes a reel:**
- **Under 34 seconds.** Your data: everything ≤37s holds 43–56%, everything ≥42s holds 8–10%. It's a cliff. The recruiter block was already flagged "get under 40s" — 40 is the edge, not the target.
- **Cut the wind-up.** Open on the object. Nothing before it.

# PART 5 — THE WEEKLY LOOP

Ten minutes a day, and Thursday is real.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Mon** | Pick the target from [target_bank.md](target_bank.md). Write only beat 1 — eight objects, eight lines. Nothing else. |
| **Tue** | Fill beats 2–3 on all eight. Command + reason only. Still no jokes. |
| **Wed** | Beats 4–5 — escalation and exits. This is where the jokes actually arrive, and they arrive easily because the frame is already standing. |
| **Thu** | Pick the floor and the crack. Read aloud against a timer. **Record.** |
| **Fri** | Cut the two strongest blocks as reels, under 34s. |

The point of splitting it by beat instead of by block: **you never sit down to "write a sermon."** You sit down to write eight objects. That's a task you can do tired.

# PART 6 — WHAT'S ACTUALLY WORKING IN THE MARKET

Straight answer: I searched properly and most of it is SEO slop — AI-written blogs quoting each other about "hook in 3 seconds." Three things survived that are worth your time.

**1. The first 3 seconds drive roughly 80% of completion variance.** Directional, not gospel (it's a blog number, not YouTube's). But it agrees with your own postmortem, where a text-card opener produced 77.1% swipe-away. Beat 1 *is* your first 3 seconds. That's why the block machine front-loads the object.

**2. What travels in Indian comedy is the shared experience, not the clever premise.** The most-shared Indian comedy reels are consistently the ones "capturing experiences everyone has had" — Amit Tandon's marriage/parenting/middle-class material is the reference case. This is the same conclusion your own numbers reached: *witnessed, not relatable.* The market and your analytics agree, which is rare enough to trust.

**3. Second-person direct address is a real, studied form — and it tells you something specific.** The mechanism: using "you" implies a *fictional* impressionable viewer, and the real audience gets the joke by recognising they're watching a parody of an authority. ([Newman, *Camera Looks, Laugh Tracks and TV Comedy*](https://www.flowjournal.org/2025/04/camera-looks/); [Breaking and Making the Fourth Wall on YouTube](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10509208.2024.2444051))

**Why that matters practically:** the irony has to be legible *inside the piece*, in the first seconds — you can't rely on the audience knowing there's a persona, which is exactly the objection that killed the "I'M RIGHT" title. Opening on the object solves it: *"that meeting you have at 1.30 today"* tells a cold viewer instantly that this is about them and that the confidence is a bit. **The form is doing the work the channel name can't do yet.**

**Study targets** (watch for the mechanism, not the jokes): **Carlin** on soft language — the authority voice, and still the manual for your signature move. **Abhishek Upmanyu** for how a specific object opens a bit. That's it — two, not ten.
