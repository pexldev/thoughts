### Mumbai

In Mumbai you build this strange connection with people. 
You go to office everyday and lets say you leave at 8 AM from your house everyday from Kharghar to reach Andheri at 10 AM. Now this city is designed in such a way that at the same time there wil be atleast 50 people, a dog, a cat, a cow and a crow going  from Kharghar  to Andheri at the same time. 
YOu take the same rickshaw from your building.
You stop at the same place near the station to smoke.
You then take the same train change the same two stations reach Andheri and from there you take your route. 

Now your parents, your wife, your children dont know that you smoke. But this one guy who has been with you through out knows exactly which brand you smoke and exactly how do u like yor chai. 

You think the meltdown you had in his rickshaw is the worst thing he's seen.

Then casually, eyes on the road —

"Aapke building mai 703 waali hai naa… us din woh to itna ro rahi thi phone mai… boli mai Vashi bridge se kood ke mar jaungi."

And you go —

oh.

Oh okay.

Suddenly your problems feel very manageable.

You were crying about a job rejection.

She was threatening to jump off a bridge.

You're fine. You're actually fine.

This man is not a rickshaw driver.

He's a therapist running 12 sessions a day at ₹180 per ride.

He knows everyone's worst moment. By flat number.

"703 waali." Not her name. Her flat.

He has a full catalogue.

And somewhere in that catalogue — you're in there too.

"Wo 402 wala… haan haan roya tha ek baar. Job wala scene tha."

That's the thing about this city.

Everyone is broken.

Everyone knows they're broken.

And there's this unspoken agreement — up to a certain point, that's okay.

You can cry in a rickshaw. You can eat vada pav alone at 11pm. You can sit on Marine Drive and stare at nothing for an hour.

Nobody will ask. Nobody will judge.

Because the person next to you is doing the same thing.

This whole city came here with a dream.

And the dream did something to all of us.

But we stay.

We get on the train the next morning. We order the chai. We show up.

That's the Mumbai contract.

(beat)

And you know you've truly become a Mumbaikar —

when you stop being surprised by your own sadness.
Because there is always someone who is more sadder than you AND no matter who you are in this city you will always feel blessed that atleast you are not situation is not that bad.


I want to somehow take it back to the fact that if i die in this city im sure that i wont have even 4 shoulders to take my dead body to the samshaanghat.

And I think that is how it should be. 
It should be like that who M I.
In this city you are just a spec infront of that ocean. 
You will always feel small infront of that ocean and you will always be small infront of that ocean.
And everyone is busy in this city to be bigger than that ocean but that ocean will consume everyone one day
